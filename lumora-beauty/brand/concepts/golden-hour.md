Brand direction for **Lumora Beauty**. Chester Studio, concept 23 Nov 2026.

- **Board:** `brand/concepts/golden-hour-board.png` (1440 px wide). The HTML source is in the session
  scratchpad at `boards/golden-hour/board.html` and is not committed.
- **Angle:** golden-hour ritual. Warm, sensorial, inclusive skin and wellbeing.
- **US-first store:** all customer-facing copy uses US spelling.

> **In one line.** Everyone looks their best in golden hour. Lumora, from *lumen* (light) and *ora* (hour), turns
> that light into a daily ritual: skincare that leaves every skin tone looking warm-lit and soft-focus, and a
> slow, warm hour you get back. Six products form one shelf that sets like the sun, from late light to dusk.

## 1. Big idea and positioning

### 1.1 Big idea: "The hour of light"

Golden hour is the one light that flatters everyone. The low sun is warm, soft and directional. It fills in
texture, warms every undertone and makes skin look lit from within, on fair and deep skin alike. It is also the
moment the day slows down.

Lumora is built on that observation, and the name already holds it: **lumen + ora = the hour of light**. The
brand promises both halves of golden hour:

1. **The look.** Skin that looks warm-lit, even and soft-focus. The glow comes from skincare (vitamin C,
   carotenoid-rich oils, barrier care, light-diffusing textures), never from mica, glitter or self-tan. The glow formulas carry no tint; Apricity's sheer color comes from iron oxides.
2. **The feeling.** Ten slow minutes. Golden hour comes twice a day (after sunrise and before sunset), so the
   ritual does too. The evening half carries on beyond the face into the body (Gloaming oil) and a warm cup
   (Slow Hour golden milk). That is the "Health" in Health & Beauty, with no health claims attached.

**Why it can be owned.** The name supplies the idea rather than it being borrowed. Three devices express it
and nobody else can claim them together:

- **The logo:** the "o" in the wordmark is a sun touching the horizon.
- **The shelf:** the six products step through a sunset, from sand to dusk, and each label's sun mark sinks one
  notch.
- **The homepage:** color schemes warm as you scroll and reach dusk at the footer.

### 1.4 Voice: warm, unhurried, exact

Lumora sounds like a friend who notices the light: calm, sensory and precise. It speaks in the second person
and uses "we" for the brand. It names the ingredient, the percentage and the hour. It does not hype.

**Principles**
- **Light and time are the shared vocabulary:** late light, low sun, the last hour, twice a day, by week four.
  Use one light word per section at most.
- **Contrast pairs carry the promise:** "Warm, never orange." "Lit, never glittery." Use one device per block.
- **Numbers beat adjectives**, and every statistic carries its footnote in the same viewport.
- **A lexicon, used lightly.** Product names are words for light, and each PDP opens with a one-line definition
  ("gloaming (n.) the soft light after sunset").
- **No exclamation marks and no emoji.** Use sentence case, the Oxford comma and US spelling.

**Sample lines**
- Brand: "Golden hour happens twice a day. So does the ritual."
- Hero: "Late light, bottled."
- PDP: "The color is the ingredient: sea buckthorn's own carotenoids. No dyes."
- Lexicon: "gloaming (n.) the soft light after sunset. Also: your shoulders, after this oil."
- Ingredients: "We leave out mica and glitter. The glow should be your skin's, not a coating."
- Cart: "You're $11 from free shipping. An Apricity would do it."
- Newsletter: "The Low Sun Letter. One note a fortnight, sent at your golden hour."
- 404: "You've wandered past sunset. Back to the light."

| We say | We never say |
|---|---|
| warm, lit, soft-focus, satin, plush, slow, late light, twice a day, looks, feels, helps | sun-kissed, bronzed, tan, after-sun, flawless, miracle, treats, repairs, heals, anti-aging, detox, clean, chemical-free |

The golden-hour theme needs a compliance guardrail. The brand never implies sun protection, tanning or
after-sun care. Ritual graphics show "your daily SPF" as an unbranded morning step.

## 3. Logo

**Wordmark:** lowercase **lumora** set in Fraunces SuperSoft at weight 380 and optical size 144, tracked -1%.
For the master artwork, outline it and redraw by hand.

- **The setting-sun o.** The "o" is cut by a horizontal gap (the horizon) at 66.5 to 71% of the glyph box,
  about one third up its height. The sliver below the horizon turns **Terracotta** on light grounds and
  **Honey** on Dusk, like a sun touching the horizon. In one-color use (labels, foil, embossing) the cut stays
  and the color doesn't change.
- **The gap** is 4.5% of the cap height, so it survives at 24 px wide. Below 80 px wide, use the one-color
  version with the gap widened to 7%.
- **Monogram, "the low sun":** a solid disc cut by the same horizon gap. It is used for the favicon, app tile,
  map pins, sun dots, the wax-seal box sticker and the label sun mark. Colorways: Honey on Dusk (primary tile),
  the golden-hour gradient on Linen, and Linen on Terracotta.
- **Descriptor lockup:** wordmark, a hairline, then BEAUTY in Figtree 600 at +28% tracking, with the optional
  line "The hour of light" in Fraunces italic.
- **Clear space:** one "o" width on every side. **Minimum size:** 72 px or 18 mm wide for the wordmark, 16 px for
  the monogram.
- **Don'ts:** no gradient fill on the whole wordmark except the giant footer sign-off, no outlines or shadows,
  no uppercase setting, and never re-type the logo in live text without the cut "o".

## 4. Art direction

**One light for renders and photographs.** Product imagery comes from our Blender (Cycles) rig in
`renders/rig/`. Skin, hands and texture come from Unsplash. One grade ties them together. Every image passes
the same test: honey highlights, dusk shadows, nothing pure black or white.

### 4.1 Packshot look (cards, PDP slot 1, cart, mega menu)

- **Backdrop:** rig `sweep` in **Sand `#E9D9C5`**. The rig color-locks the backdrop to the hex, so the sweep
  renders exactly as the card tile and packshots sit seamlessly in Minimog cards. There is no multiply-blend
  hack.
- **Light:** rig preset `soft_studio` with `kelvin_shift: -500` (key about 4,800 K). A soft warm key from the
  left (-52°), a warm rim each side, and the backdrop light making a soft halo behind glass.
- **Camera:** `pdp_4x5` (90 mm, 6° elevation, product fills 72% of the height), label square to camera, centered.
  Every product is rendered at the same camera height and scale family. Output is 2048 px on the long edge.
- **Card hover image:** the same pack, camera and crop in low sun (4.2). "The light changes" on hover.

### 4.2 Low-sun look (hover images, hero and homepage scenes)

- **Light:** rig preset `sunlit` plus `sun: {az: -60, el: 12, kelvin: 3300, angle: 1.2}` and
  `gobo: {pattern: "leaves", coverage: 0.7, leaf_scale: 1.1}`. This gives hard, raking, warm light with olive
  leaf shadows.
- **Sets:** travertine plinths (rig `block`, `step` and `cylinder` shapes in `travertine`), at most one glass
  sphere, an olive sprig, and plaster for the evening scenes.
- **The signature shot:** amber glass throws its own color into its shadow, so every amber product has a
  glowing orange core inside its cast shadow on the wall or plinth. Keep the rig's glass shadow on and add
  caustics for hero scenes only.
- **Backdrop colors for scenes:** wall `#E7D2BC` in shade and `#F4C996` where the sun falls.

### 4.4 Unsplash photography brief

- **Subjects:** skin in late sun, at close and medium crops: cheekbones, jawlines, collarbones, shoulders,
  backs of necks, hands and wrists. Also hands holding a warm cup. Leaf or window-light shadows falling across
  skin. For textures: apricot halves, sea buckthorn berries, honey and oil drops, linen in sun, terracotta,
  travertine.
- **Light:** low sun (side or back light), warm (about 3,000 to 3,800 K), with hard-edged leaf shadows. Some
  flare is fine. Shoot (or select) indoor window light at golden hour as often as outdoor light.
- **Casting:** Fitzpatrick I to VI in every set of four, ages 25 to 60, all genders. Real texture: pores, fine
  hairs, freckles, stretch marks and hyperpigmentation are kept. Minimal makeup, eyes closed or in profile,
  candid rather than posed.
- **Search terms:** "golden hour skin", "leaf shadow portrait", "sunlight on skin", "hands sunlight shadow",
  "window light portrait warm", "turmeric latte", "apricot", "sea buckthorn".
- **Avoid:** beaches, swimwear, sunbathing, tanning or anything implying sun care; body glitter or shimmer;
  heavily retouched or filtered skin; teal-orange or cool grades; silhouettes with no skin detail; visible
  brands, logos or tattoos with text; glossy stock smiles at camera; any image that could pass as a customer
  before-and-after.
- **Crops:** 4:5 for portraits and cards, 3:4 for gallery tiles, 16:9 only for bands. Record the photographer
  and URL in `photos/CREDITS.md`.

### 4.5 The grade: "Lumora Golden Hour"

Apply one LUT to every photo and every render.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| White balance | Warm by about +500 K, tint +4 toward magenta |
| Highlights | Tinted toward Honey `#F2CB8C` at 12% |
| Shadows | Tinted toward Dusk plum `#3A2422` at 15% |
| Black point | Lifted to `#2A1A18` (nothing is pure black) |
| White point | Capped at `#FBF3EA` (nothing is pure white) |
| Saturation | -8% overall, with the orange-red band (skin and amber glass) protected; orange luminance +6 |
| Clarity | -5 for soft focus, never on product labels |
| Grain and halation | 35 mm grain at 2 to 3%, and a faint warm halation on speculars |

For renders, set the rig to AgX "Medium High Contrast" at exposure +0.2, then apply the same LUT.

**Test:** place a render and a portrait side by side. They must share one highlight hue, and skin must stay on
the skin-tone line.

### 4.6 Motifs, texture, dividers and icons

- **Sun marks (the step system).** An arc above a horizon line, with a sun dot that sinks from step 01 (high
  left) to 06 (half set). They appear on labels, product cards, the PDP eyebrow, the ritual section and the
  mega menu.
- **Leaf light.** The rig's procedural `leaves` gobo mask is exported as a PNG and used on the web at 6%
  multiply on two sections at most. These are the same leaves that fall on the packshots.
- **Horizon edge (signature transition).** A shallow convex SVG arc, like the rim of the sun or the curve of the
  horizon. It is used under the hero into the promise strip, and from the closing band into the footer, and
  nowhere else.
- **Glow.** One radial honey-to-apricot bloom behind the hero products and the closing band.
- **Icons.** A custom 1.4 px monoline set on a 32 px grid, with rounded caps and joins. Every icon sits on a
  short horizon hairline. The set: shipping, guarantee (with a sun dot), every skin tone (three overlapping
  circles), amber glass, vegan formula, golden hour, drop (fragrance-free), and how-to-use steps. Icons are
  Dusk on light grounds and Linen on dark.
- **Shape and motion.** One radius token: 14 px for cards, tiles, images and drawers; 999 px for buttons,
  inputs and chips. One easing curve, `cubic-bezier(.25,.8,.25,1)` ("slow sunset"), at 200 ms and 600 ms. Images
  zoom to 1.04 over 900 ms on hover. Everything is switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 5. The line: six products, one sunset shelf

**Shelf logic:**
- **Constants:** amber glass, champagne brushed metal and one label system.
- **One sunset tone per product**, stepping across the line: Sand, Honey, Apricot, Terracotta, Clay, Dusk.
  In order on a shelf, the six read like the sky from late light to dusk.
- **Sun mark:** every label carries one, sinking a notch per step.
- **Silhouettes:** the heights alternate (150, 115, 65, 82, 166 and 94 mm), so the line-up has a skyline.

**Label system** (screen print unless noted):
1. The wordmark with the cut "o".

## 9. Risks of this direction, and how we handle them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Sun imagery can read as sun care, tanning or after-sun**, which is drug and claim territory and off-brief | No beaches, swimwear or bronzed skin. Banned words: sun-kissed, bronzed, after-sun. "Your daily SPF" appears as a morning step. Glow language stays appearance-only. |
| **Warm-paper palette next to the references.** Coldbrook uses rust on paper and Convenz saffron on paper, so we could read as "another warm Minimog store". | A rosier Terracotta (7° vs 18°), a soft serif instead of condensed caps, plum-brown Dusk ink, gradients and glow, and the beauty-specific scheme ladder. No torn edges and no all-caps headlines. |
| **"Glow" is a crowded beauty trope** | Own the system, not the word: the lexicon names, sun marks, the sunset shelf and the setting wordmark. The promise is specific ("warm, never orange; lit, never glittery"). |
| **Gradients and glows can look cheap or "filtered"** | At most four gradient uses; low chroma; 2% grain against banding; never on text; never teal-orange. |
| **An amber dropper is a familiar apothecary look** | Champagne collar, Honey bulb, the cut-"o" wordmark, the sun mark, and the amber-shadow signature in every scene. |
| **Inclusivity must be shown, not claimed** | Casting across Fitzpatrick I to VI in every image set, three Apricity shades, a footnoted finish test (placeholder), and no single "universal shade" claim. |
| **Slow Hour (ingestible) brings food rules and could feel off-brief to a beauty-minded client** | Sold as a spice blend with no health or supplement claims and food labeling. A ready fallback (Afterlight Eye Balm in the same stick format) keeps the shelf if the client prefers cosmetics only. |
| **Fraunces is popular in DTC (food and lifestyle)** | Use the SuperSoft optical size, light weights at display sizes, true WONK italics, and a custom wordmark that isn't typed live. |
| **Hard light can hide labels and slow the renders** | Cards use soft studio light; low sun is only for hover images and scenes. Caustics only on hero renders; preview at 48 samples first. |
| **Unsplash supply.** Consistent warm-light skin across tones is uneven, and model releases are not guaranteed. | Shortlist 20 to get 8, grade every one, crop to 4:5, and avoid recognizable faces for a live launch (or license them). |
| **The golden-hour clock and the scroll ladder need JS and editor discipline** | Both degrade gracefully: the clock hides if it can't compute, and the ladder is just schemes. Test at 390, 768 and 1440 with no horizontal overflow. |
| **Washes reduce contrast for secondary text** | Scheme table (2.2) with checked pairs. Clay or Dusk for small accent text on washes, Honey only at 24 px and up on Terracotta or Clay. |

## Appendix B. Golden-hour clock (Custom Liquid sketch)

- **Input:** `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`, mapped through a table of about 40 time zones
  to a representative city's latitude and longitude. The fallback is New York. No geolocation prompt and no
  cookies.
- **Maths:** the NOAA solar-position algorithm, run minute by minute over today. The evening window starts when
  solar elevation falls through +6° and ends when it falls through -4°. The morning window uses the same
  thresholds rising.
- **Output:** "Golden hour in {city} today: {start} to {end}". After sunset it reads "Next golden hour:
  {tomorrow morning}". In polar or edge cases it hides itself.
- **Check:** on 23 Nov 2026 the formula gives New York 3:51 to 4:50 pm (sunset 4:33 pm), Chicago 3:42 to
  4:42 pm and Los Angeles 4:09 to 5:01 pm.
