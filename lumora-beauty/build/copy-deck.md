### 16.2 Why amber *(in snippet)*

- Why amber
- The bottle does half the *work.*
- Vitamin C and the carotenoids in sea buckthorn both fade in light. So we chose a stable form of vitamin C, then put it in amber glass, which filters most of the light that breaks them down. A slim dropper lets in less air than a jar or a pump, so the last drop is as good as the first.
- Vitamin C left after 12 weeks in daylight³
- Amber glassLong Light’s bottle
- 97%
- Clear glassSame formula
- 82%
- A dropper, not a pump
- Less air in, and you measure every drop.
- Six months once open
- One bottle lasts about ten weeks, twice a day.
- ³Stability test: vitamin C content by HPLC after 12 weeks under daylight-equivalent light at 25 °C, the same formula in amber and in clear glass. Illustrative placeholder data.

- No. The apricot in the bottle is sea buckthorn oil at 2%, and it sinks in clear within a minute. Our expert panel saw no orange cast, gray cast or shimmer on 58 of 60 people, across Fitzpatrick I to VI¹.
- Will it suit sensitive skin?
- It’s fragrance-free and free of essential oils, and this form of vitamin C works at a skin-friendly pH. If your skin is reactive, patch-test on your inner arm for two evenings, then start every other evening and build up to twice a day.
- Can I use it with retinol, acids or niacinamide?
- Yes, with a little planning. Use Long Light in the morning and retinol at night. With exfoliating acids, alternate evenings or keep the acids to the evening. Niacinamide and hyaluronic serums layer freely, thinnest texture first.

- Not once it has sunk in. Sea buckthorn oil is naturally deep orange, so give it a minute before you press your face into a white pillowcase or towel. On skin, it sinks in clear.

- About ten weeks at 3 to 4 drops, twice a day, and the 15 ml travel size about five. Once open, use it within six months.

- Long Light is formulated without retinoids or essential oils. Even so, check with your doctor before you start any new skincare while pregnant or breastfeeding.
