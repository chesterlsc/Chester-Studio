Why amber

The bottle is part of the formula.

Vitamin C and the carotenoids in sea buckthorn both fade in light. A stable form of vitamin C does half the work. Amber glass does the rest: it filters most of the light that breaks them down, and a slim dropper lets in less air than a jar or a pump. So the last drop is as good as the first.

  • A dropper, not a pumpLess air in, and you measure every drop.
  • Six months once openOne bottle lasts about ten weeks, twice a day.

Two actives do the visible work. The other thirteen keep them stable, comfortable and easy to wear, listed here in label order with what each one is for.

  • Drying alcoholsThey can leave skin feeling tight after a few minutes.

How to use

Three steps, under a minute.

Morning and evening, after Sunmelt and before Haze. The whole ritual takes ten slow minutes; this part takes one.

  1. 01

    Warm

    Shake 3 to 4 drops into your palm and warm them between your hands for a few seconds.

  2. 02

    Press

    Press into clean, slightly damp skin: face, neck and the backs of your hands. Pressing spreads it evenly, without pulling.

  3. 03

    Layer

    Give it 30 seconds, then follow with Haze. In the morning, finish with your daily SPF.

Who it’s for, honestly.

  • Dry
  • Normal

Made for

  • Skin that looks dull or tired, especially by the afternoon
  • Tone that looks uneven, and dark marks that linger
  • Every skin tone, from Fitzpatrick I to VI1
  • A first vitamin C: start every other evening

Maybe not for you if

  • You want a tint or a shimmer. It leaves neither.
  • Your skin has reacted to vitamin C before, even stable forms.
  • You want your serum to protect you from the sun. It doesn’t: keep your SPF.

Good to know

  • Fragrance-free and free of essential oils
  • Vegan formula, with squalane from olives
  • Formulated without retinoids. Pregnant or breastfeeding? Check with your doctor first.
