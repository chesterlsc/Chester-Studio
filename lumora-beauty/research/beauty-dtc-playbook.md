## 0. The short version (12 rules)

1. **Sell one ritual, not six products.** Six SKUs make a system. Number the steps, show the whole range in one grid, and make the "start here" product obvious.
2. **Feel first, then prove.** Lead with a sensory headline, then name the ingredient and its percentage, then give a footnoted result.
3. **Every number carries its footnote in the same viewport.** In this trial, label every statistic as illustrative placeholder data.
4. **Build trust into the page instead of adding badges.** Show a founder or formulator with a face, the full ingredient list as text, a plain-English guarantee, a real way to reach a person, and a one-line summary of each policy.
5. **Never fake proof.** No invented reviews presented as real, no press logos, no certification marks we don't hold, no fake scarcity, no live-viewer counters.
6. **The PDP is where we beat the bar.** The PDPs we sampled on the reference stores are close to stock Minimog. Ours gets a structured buy box and eight to ten storytelling modules, which is the "depth" the brief asks for.
7. **One primary action per screen.** Every other action is a text link.
8. **Design for mobile first.** Use 4:5 media, a sticky add-to-bag bar, 44 px tap targets and short headlines, and put each footnote directly under its claim.
9. **Make cosmetic claims only.** Talk about how skin looks and feels ("visibly", "the look of", "helps", "feels"). Never "treats", "heals", "repairs", "boosts collagen" or "anti-inflammatory".
10. **Restraint reads as premium.** Two typefaces, one accent colour, one icon style, at most one badge per product card, no strikethrough prices.
11. **No dead ends.** Only two pages are in scope, so every link must land somewhere deliberate: a homepage anchor, the built PDP, or the polished default PDP that the other five products use.
12. **Write product facts as text.** Screen readers, search engines and AI shopping assistants read text and structured data, not pixels. Since December 2025, Shopify's Agentic Storefronts have fed catalogues to ChatGPT, Copilot, Google AI Mode and Gemini.

## 1. What the client's references tell us

We fetched the homepage and product-page HTML of all three references on 23 Nov 2026.

**Platform.** All three run **Minimog OS 2.0 v5.9.0**. Each adds custom sections on top: a global "chrome skin" style section, custom hero, promise, collection, bento and ritual sections, and an oversized footer wordmark. The section naming conventions match across the three sites, which suggests one team built them all. Their polish comes from a disciplined design system applied over Minimog, not from stock demo sections. That is the bar.

**What their homepages share:**

- **Announcement bar.** One line covering the shipping threshold and returns, for example "Free US shipping over $70 · 30-day returns".
- **Hero.** A category eyebrow, a two-to-four-word headline, a one-sentence promise and two CTAs. Inzinnergy also puts a product card (name and price) inside the hero.
- **Promise strip.** Sits directly under the hero. Three or four points, each with a sub-line: shipping, returns, secure checkout and one product-specific point.
- **Small-range confidence.** The range is presented as deliberately small: "a small range, chosen with care", "the range stays small on purpose".
- **Kit storytelling.** Pieces are numbered 01–06, and you can buy them one at a time or as a set.
- **Copy.** Plain, specific and full of numbers, with no hype. "Honest specs" is an explicit value.
- **Other modules:** a benefit marquee, a benefit bento grid, a "complete the ritual" cross-sell, and testimonials with a headline per quote plus first name and initial.
- **Ending.** A closing CTA band, a conversational FAQ, a footer with a one-sentence brand description, a newsletter written in the brand voice, an oversized wordmark and a mobile sticky bar.

**Their PDPs are much thinner.** Inzinnergy and Coldbrook use the stock main-product section with a long description and spec lists, a review list with a rating distribution, and recommendations. This is the clearest place for Lumora to visibly exceed the bar.

**What to take from this:**

- **Match the qualities, not the layouts.** Take the clarity, the small-range confidence, the honest specifics, the trust strip, the kit storytelling and the wordmark footer, and express them in a beauty idiom: light, skin, texture, ritual.
- **Do not reuse their sentence patterns.** For example, avoid "N pieces. One better X." or "Good questions." The client built or chose these sites and will recognise echoes.
- **Make review content specific.** If the build shows reviews, each one must be specific, varied and visibly labelled as sample content. When the same sentence appears across several "different" reviewers, an expert reads it as generated, and on a live store it is illegal (section 7.8).

**What these sites have in common in 2025–2026:**

- A quiet, editorial interface with the product as hero, large display type and one signature colour.
- Macro texture photography and short looping videos.
- Barrier-first messaging, with irritation as proof of efficacy falling out of favour.
- "Fewer products that do more" for cautious, value-aware shoppers.
- Ingredient transparency, numbered routines, minis and refills.
- Shoppable creator video.

## 3. Homepage: narrative and section order

### 3.1 The job of the page

The brand is new, has six products and no reviews. In about 60 seconds the homepage has to convince a stranger of three things: this is a real, careful brand; its formulas will suit their skin; and there is a low-risk first step to take.

Each section does one job in that arc:

| Job | Sections |
|---|---|
| Promise | Hero |
| Reassure | Promise strip |
| Orient | The collection |
| Focus | Signature product |
| Believe | Ingredients |
| Prove | Results |
| Expand | Ritual |
| Humanise | Founder or formulator |
| Belong | Early voices |
| De-risk | FAQ and guarantee |
| Stay | Newsletter and footer |

### 3.2 Section order, with the reasoning

The core is 11 body sections plus header and footer. The marquee is optional.

**Rules for every section:**

- Headline of six words or fewer.
- Body copy of 35 words or fewer.
- One CTA.
- Each product appears at most twice on the page.

### 3.4 Leave these off the homepage

- Press-logo walls ("As seen in…") and award seals. We have neither.
- A live Instagram feed widget (it breaks and looks empty) and a blog section with no posts.
- Countdown timers, spin-to-win wheels, pop-ups on entry, "X people are viewing", and "someone just bought" toasts.
- Stars on product cards before there are reviews, and "Bestseller" badges on a brand-new line.
- An autoplaying multi-slide hero.
- Two carousels showing the same six products.
- A "Shop by concern" grid (it duplicates the collection when there are only six SKUs), and a skin quiz. A quiz is overkill for six products. The ritual section does that job.

### 3.5 Rhythm and polish rules

- **Alternate the background.** Use three tones (paper, mist, and one deep tone for at most one or two sections), and alternate image-left and image-right.
- **Pace the text.** Never put more than two text-heavy sections back to back. Follow a dense module (ingredients, results) with an image-led one.
- **One spacing scale.** Suggested section padding: 120/96 px on desktop, 64/56 px on mobile. Use the same internal gaps everywhere.
- **Pair two typefaces.** One display (an editorial serif or a refined grotesk) and one text sans. Avoid long runs of all-caps. Use sentence case for headings and buttons.
- **Keep the art direction consistent.** One light direction, one colour grade, one ground colour for packshots, and real skin texture (no plastic retouching).

## 4. Product page: the conversion stack

### 4.1 Template strategy for the trial (do this first)

- **Signature template.** Build a dedicated product template (for example `product.signature`) for Halo Serum with every module. Only that product uses it.
- **Default template.** Make it polished but generic: gallery, title, a subtitle from a metafield, benefits from metafields, price, variant, Add to bag, trust row, accordions fed by metafields, "Complete the ritual" and recommendations. It must not contain product-specific static copy, or the other five products will show Halo Serum's claims.
- **Fill minimum metafields for all six products:** subtitle, three benefits, key ingredients, how to use, texture or finish, and "suited to". The reviewer will click other products from the homepage grid, and none of them may look empty.
- **Inventory and prices.** Turn inventory tracking off (or allow overselling) so nothing shows "Sold out". Give every product a realistic placeholder price. A "$0.00" or "Free" price looks broken and breaks the free-shipping logic. Leave compare-at prices empty.

### 4.2 Layout

- **Desktop (1440).** A 12-column grid. The gallery takes 7 columns, either as a stacked two-up editorial grid or as a main image with vertical thumbnails. Baymard recommends visible thumbnails on desktop. The buy box takes 5 columns and is sticky (top offset = header height + 24 px), so Add to bag stays in reach while the gallery scrolls. Full-width storytelling modules follow below, alternating backgrounds.
- **Mobile (390).** A full-bleed swipe gallery, then the buy box, then the modules. A sticky add-to-bag bar appears once the main button scrolls away.

- **Header line:** "Your bag (2)".
- **Free-shipping progress bar** in brand colours: "You're $12 from free shipping" changes to "Free shipping unlocked". Use FoxKit's free shipping goal or the theme's own cart goal.
- **Line items** with size and step number, and quantity steppers styled as pills.

### 4.8 Subscribe and save, done right

- **Default to one-time purchase,** with the subscription card visually highlighted. A pre-selected subscription is a consent risk. California's amended Automatic Renewal Law (in force since 1 July 2025) requires express affirmative consent, and the EU's proposed Digital Fairness Act (adopted by the Commission on 11 November 2026) targets subscription traps.
- **Match the cadence to usage:** "Every 8 weeks (about one bottle)". Offer 4, 6, 8 and 12 weeks.
- **Put the terms next to the button:** price per delivery, frequency, that it renews automatically, and "Skip, pause or cancel anytime in your account". Cancellation must be as easy as sign-up (California).
- **Discount:** 10–15% is the category norm. Don't stack it with the bundle discount.
- **Build:** the Shopify Subscriptions app (or Recharge, Skio or Loop) and its app block, or Minimog's selling-plan support if the capability map confirms it.

### 4.9 Bundles, thresholds and basket-size maths

- **Price ladder (illustrative):** Lip Veil $18 · Dawn Cleansing Milk $32 · Dew Essence $34 · Lumen Eye Gel $38 · Halo Serum $48 · Afterglow Cream $52.
- **Threshold.** Set it 20–30% above the hero product's price ($60 against a $48 serum). The cheapest product ($18) bridges the gap: $48 + $18 = $66 qualifies. That is the job of a lip SKU, as Rhode, Laneige and Summer Fridays show.
- **Bundles.** The full ritual (six products) at 15% off, plus a two-product "Serum and cream" pair at 10% off. Show the saving in dollars and per cent. Bundles are cleaner than tiered "buy 2, get 10%" discounts for a regimen brand.
- **Discounts.** Avoid stacked discounts and strikethrough prices on a launch. In the EU, any "was" price must reference the lowest price of the previous 30 days.

### 4.10 Reviews for a brand that has none

- **In the trial.** Either use a review app with imported sample reviews and a visible label ("Sample reviews shown for this demo store"), or a static Custom Liquid module styled to match.
  - Write 12–20 varied, specific reviews with a realistic spread: an average of about 4.6–4.7, including three- and four-star reviews that name real trade-offs ("wish it came with a pump", "took two weeks to notice").
  - Never repeat sentences across reviewers.
- **For a real launch:**
  - **Seed with a disclosed sampling programme.** Every review from it carries "Received product free". Incentives must never depend on sentiment.
  - **Ask post-purchase at day 14–21,** when results are visible, and prompt for photos and skin type.
  - **Show negative reviews and reply in public.**
  - **Publish a short review policy.** The UK CMA expects one, and the EU requires traders to say whether and how they verify reviews.
- **Display:** a filter for skin type is the single most useful beauty-specific feature. Show photo reviews first and a "what people mention" summary (human or AI-assisted).

## 5. Trust without review history

Each tactic below includes how to show it honestly in the trial.

1. **A named standard for formulas.** Publish "The Lumora standard": fragrance-free, the exclusions with the reason for each, and the testing each formula gets. A concrete, defined standard holds up with regulators and in court far better than "clean" (see 7.6).
2. **Full ingredient lists as text, decoded.** Give the INCI list on every PDP with a plain-English line per ingredient (Byoma-style), plus the percentages of the hero actives (The Ordinary-style).
3. **A human face.** A founder or formulator note with a portrait and signature, and a named role ("Formulated with independent cosmetic chemists"). In the trial, use a clearly fictional persona or a role-based placeholder.
4. **Results with the method attached.** Use consumer-perception and instrumental results with numbered footnotes (n, duration, population, method). Mark them "Illustrative placeholder data" in the trial.
5. **Badges that would plausibly be true.** Show them as plain text with an icon, not certification logos:
   - Fragrance-free and vegan formula (true by formulation).
   - Dermatologist-tested and non-comedogenic tested (footnoted test method).
   - Ophthalmologist-tested (eye gel only).
   - Cruelty-free, as text only, unless certified (see 7.5).
6. **Risk reversal.** "30 days to love it, even if it's empty", free returns, a travel size and a free deluxe sample. This lowers the cost of trying a new brand.
7. **Operational transparency.** Shipping costs and the threshold up front, a calculated delivery estimate, a real contact email with response time, and a one-line summary of each policy.
8. **Disclosed early voices.** Tester quotes and creator texture videos, each marked "received product free" or #ad. Label them as sample content in the trial.
9. **Expert voice.** A short note from the formulator or dermatologist. It must be real for a live store and labelled as a placeholder in the trial.
10. **Education.** "How to layer", "what 10% niacinamide does", and a results timeline. Expertise shown on the page beats expertise claimed.
11. **The site's own polish.** In Stanford's web credibility research, design and look was the cue people mentioned most when judging credibility (about 46% of comments). Consistent art direction, typography and zero broken states are trust signals in themselves.
12. **Recognised payment brands and branded checkout.** Familiar wallets and a checkout that matches the store remove last-step doubt.
13. **No fake signals.** Nothing destroys trust (or invites enforcement) faster than invented press, reviews or certifications, or fake urgency.

## 6. Copywriting principles

### 6.1 Voice

The voice is a knowledgeable friend: calm, precise, warm. Not clinical coldness, not influencer hype.

- Confidence comes from specifics, not superlatives.
- Use second person ("your skin") and "we" for the brand.
- Plain-spoken like the client's references, but softer and more sensory.

### 6.2 Sensory lexicon (pick, don't pile up)

- **Texture:** water-light, gel-cream, milky, cushiony, bouncy, silky, whipped, balm-to-oil, jelly.
- **On skin:** sinks in, melts, glides, no tack, no residue, cool, plush, comfortable.
- **Finish:** dewy, satin, soft-matte, glassy, velvet, lit from within, rested.
- **Scent:** "nothing added. What you smell is the formula: faintly green, gone in seconds."
- **Ritual and time:** one pump, three drops, in 30 seconds, by morning, by week four.
- **Light motif (on brand):** catches the light, luminous, soft-focus, halo, first light, afterglow. Use one per section at most.

### 6.3 Specificity rules

- Name the ingredient **and** its job **and** (for actives) its percentage: "10% niacinamide evens the look of tone." Not "powerful actives".
- Quantify sensory claims where it's honest: "absorbs in seconds", "two pumps for face and neck".
- Replace adjectives with evidence: not "luxurious texture", but "a gel that turns to water on contact".
- Each benefit answers "so what?": "Layers without pilling, so it works under SPF and makeup."

### 6.4 Rhythm

- **Headlines:** two to six words, often a fragment ("Light work for skin.").
- **Subheads:** one sentence, 22 words or fewer.
- **Paragraphs:** three lines or fewer on mobile.
- **Vary sentence length:** short, short, then a longer sentence that lands the benefit.
- **Use triads and contrast pairs** ("Water-light, not watery." "Dewy, never greasy."), but no more than one device per block.
- **Read every block aloud.** If you run out of breath, cut.

### 6.5 Product naming conventions

**What a good name is:**

1. Short: one or two words plus a format word (Serum, Cream, Gel).
2. Evokes a moment or feeling (light, dawn, dew, glow), never a claim.
3. Part of a system: a shared motif and a step number (01–06) give the six products family logic, the way Medik8's strength steps and Summer Fridays' routines do.
4. Paired with a **descriptor line** that gives format, hero ingredient and percentage: "10% niacinamide + rice ferment".
5. Distinct on a two-column mobile grid: different first words and different lengths.
6. **Claim-safe.** No "Repair", "Heal", "Rx", "MD", "Derm", "Clinical", "Acne" or "Lift" in a product name. Names are labelling, and labelling claims count.
7. Free of obvious trademark collisions (avoid famous names such as Cloud Paint, Jet Lag or Glazing).

**For variants:** shade or flavour names follow one template, a name plus a sensory hint ("Lip Veil, Dusk: sheer rose, nothing added").

**Recommended system for Lumora:** a step number, a light-moment name, the format, and the ingredient descriptor. For example "03 · Halo Serum · 10% niacinamide + rice ferment". The illustrative line-up at the top follows this pattern. The brand book makes the final call.

### 6.6 PDP copy formula (per product)

1. **What it is,** in one sentence (format plus who it's for).
2. **What it does:** three benefits, appearance and feel only.
3. **Why it's different:** the ingredient, the percentage, the texture or the system.
4. **How it feels:** texture, finish, scent.
5. **How to use:** amount, when, where in the ritual, and layering.
6. **Good to know:** formulated without, suitability, patch-test note.

### 6.8 Style sheet

- **Case:** sentence case for headings and buttons. Product names in title case. Ingredient names in lower case ("niacinamide"), INCI as declared.
- **Numbers and units:** numerals throughout. "10%" with no space. "30 ml" with lower-case ml and a space. "1.0 fl oz". En dash for ranges ("2–4 weeks").
- **Spelling:** US spelling if the store is US-first. Never mix spellings.
- **Punctuation:** no exclamation marks, no emoji. Oxford comma (US).
- **Footnotes:** numbered superscripts (¹ ² ³), linked to the footnote block and to the copy directly beneath the claim.

### 6.9 Rewrites (generic to Lumora)

- "Unlock radiant, glowing skin with our luxurious serum!" → "A water-light serum with 10% niacinamide. Skin looks smoother by day 7², more even by week four²."
- "Deeply nourishes and repairs your skin barrier." → "Ceramides and cholesterol help replenish the moisture barrier, so tight, dry-feeling skin feels comfortable again, all day."
- "Clinically proven to reduce wrinkles by 50%." → "Fine lines look visibly softer at 8 weeks.⁴" Footnote: "Expert visual grading, 30 participants, 8 weeks. Illustrative placeholder data."
- "Clean, non-toxic, chemical-free formula." → "Formulated without fragrance, essential oils or drying alcohols. Every ingredient is listed and explained below."

### 6.10 Words to retire

Elevate, unlock, indulge, luxurious (show it instead), game-changer, holy grail, miracle, magic, revolutionary, flawless, perfect skin, fix your flaws, anti-ageing (use "visible signs of ageing" sparingly, or better "skin that looks rested"), detox, clean (undefined), non-toxic, chemical-free.

## 7. Claims compliance (US, EU, UK)

### 7.1 How the line is drawn

- **US (FD&C Act, FDA, FTC).** What a product is depends on its **intended use**, and claims establish intended use.
  - A **cosmetic** cleanses, beautifies, promotes attractiveness or alters the appearance.
  - A **drug** treats, cures, mitigates or prevents disease, or affects the structure or function of the body.
  - Claims count wherever they appear: the label, website copy, product names, social posts and customer testimonials we repost or endorse. FDA warning letters have cited all of these, including SEO metadata and a brand "liking" a customer's comment.
  - Recurring warning-letter themes: "collagen-building or boosting", "anti-inflammatory", "repairs cells or DNA", "reduces wrinkles" (as structure or function), "lifting like injectables", "stem cells", "eczema, acne or rosacea treatment".
  - The FTC separately requires **competent and reliable scientific evidence** for objective claims.
- **EU.** Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 Art. 20 plus Regulation (EU) 655/2013, which sets six common criteria for claims: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness and informed decision-making.
  - The Commission's claims Technical Document adds annexes on **"free from"** (Annex III) and **"hypoallergenic"** (Annex IV) claims.
  - Medicinal claims ("heals", "anti-acne treatment") take a product out of cosmetics altogether.
- **UK.** The retained cosmetics regulation (the same claims criteria) plus the CAP Code (substantiation, and section 12 for health and beauty).
  - ASA guidance on cosmetics production techniques and its 2021 rulings on beauty filters: no technique may exaggerate what the product can do.
- **Canada (useful reference).** The Ad Standards and Health Canada guidelines list acceptable non-therapeutic claims next to unacceptable therapeutic ones in two columns. It's a fast sanity check for wording.

### 7.2 Word list: red, amber, green, with safe swaps

**Red: don't use (drug or medical claims, or claims we can't stand behind):**
treat, cure, heal, prevent (a disease) · eczema, psoriasis, rosacea, dermatitis, acne treatment · anti-inflammatory · antibacterial, antimicrobial, antiseptic · boosts or stimulates collagen or elastin · regenerates or repairs cells, DNA repair, increases cell turnover · reverses ageing, erases or eliminates wrinkles, permanent · Botox-like, injectable results, non-surgical facelift · skin lightening, whitening, bleaching · SPF, sun protection, UV protection, blue-light protection (sunscreen is an OTC drug in the US) · hair growth, stops hair loss · detoxifies · medical-grade, prescription-strength · non-toxic, chemical-free · allergy-free, 100% safe, no side effects · pregnancy-safe · reef-safe · eco-friendly, green, sustainable, planet-friendly, carbon-neutral (generic environmental claims, banned in the EU since 27 Sep 2026) · unsupported "#1" or "best".

**Amber: only with evidence and the right qualifier:**
clinically tested (say what was tested) · dermatologist-tested (usually an HRIPT under dermatological supervision, commonly 50–200 panellists) · ophthalmologist-tested (eye products) · non-comedogenic (needs comedogenicity testing) · suitable for sensitive skin · hypoallergenic (EU: only if designed to minimise allergenic potential, with evidence, never implying zero risk) · "72-hour hydration" and other time-bound claims (instrumental kinetics) · "plumps" (temporary, tested) · "for blemish-prone skin" · "calms the look of redness" · "% naturally derived (ISO 16128)" · vegan (no legal definition, so audit beeswax, lanolin, carmine, animal collagen and shark squalene) · cruelty-free (certify) · "clinically proven" (only with a controlled clinical study; default to "clinically tested").

**Green: cosmetic and safe:**
cleanses, removes makeup · hydrates, moisturises · softens, smooths, conditions · brightens dull-looking skin · evens the look of skin tone · fades the look of dark spots · visibly reduces the look of fine lines · skin looks firmer, plumper, more rested · helps replenish the moisture barrier · gently exfoliates to reveal smoother-looking skin · refines the look of pores · comforts dry-feeling skin · absorbs quickly · lightweight, non-greasy · layers under makeup.

### 7.3 Statistics, studies and footnotes

- **Marker.** Every statistic gets a numbered superscript **next to the number**. The footnote text sits **directly beneath the module**, in the same viewport on mobile, at 12 px or more with AA contrast. It is repeated in the footer footnote block.
  - FTC disclosure guidance requires "clear and conspicuous": close to the claim, unavoidable, not hidden behind a link.
- **What a footnote says:** the study type (consumer perception, instrumental measurement or expert grading), n, population (ages, skin types), duration and usage frequency, which product(s) were used (alone or as a regimen), whether results were self-assessed or measured, and whether the lab was independent.
- **Honest rounding.** Report the exact figure or round down. Say "9 in 10" only at 90% or above. State the time point. Don't present "up to" as typical.
- **Templates (trial):**
  - ¹ Human repeat insult patch test under dermatological supervision, 52 participants including self-declared sensitive skin. *Illustrative placeholder claim.*
  - ² Consumer perception study, 32 participants aged 24–58 with dull-looking skin, assessed after 7 and 28 days of twice-daily use. Self-assessed. *Illustrative placeholder data.*
  - ³ Instrumental measurement (corneometer), 20 participants, one hour after a single application, against an untreated area. *Illustrative placeholder data.*
  - ⁴ Expert visual grading, 30 participants, 8 weeks of twice-daily use. *Illustrative placeholder data.*
- **Global demo label** in the footer: "Lumora is a concept store built for a design trial. Product data, results and reviews are illustrative."

### 7.4 Images

- **No before-and-after pairs unless they are real and comparable.** That means same light, angle, distance and time of day, no makeup, no filters, and the time point and usage disclosed. For the trial, use texture, application and timeline graphics instead.
- **Retouching.** Retouching that changes the skin result being claimed (smoothing pores on a "refines pores" claim) is misleading under UK ASA guidance and FTC principles. Cleaning up dust or a label is fine.
- **AI-generated people** must not pose as customers or testers. Use them only as clear lifestyle illustration, if at all. Prefer licensed photography graded to the brand look.

### 7.5 Badges, certifications, seals and press

- **Certification marks are licensed trademarks.** Leaping Bunny, Vegan Society, B Corp, EWG Verified, NEA Seal, Credo and retailer "clean" seals may only be shown under licence. **Never in the trial.** Use plain-text badges with our own icons for facts that are true by formulation.
- **No press or award logos without real coverage.** No "As seen in" for a new brand.
- **"Dermatologist recommended" requires evidence that dermatologists recommend it,** typically a survey. Use "dermatologist-tested" (with a footnote) instead.
- **"Not tested on animals."** The EU and UK ban animal testing for cosmetics, so the claim must not imply a special benefit. A recognised certification is the safe route.

### 7.6 Loaded words: clean, natural, free-from, hypoallergenic

- **"Clean" and "natural"** have no FDA definition. US class actions keep coming: an Ulta "Conscious Beauty" suit filed July 2025, PFAS-based "clean" suits in 2025, and a 2024 Sephora "Clean at Sephora" dismissal. Courts and regulators are more comfortable when "clean" is **defined** by a published exclusion list. If we say "natural", quantify it ("98% naturally derived, ISO 16128").
- **"Free from" in the EU and UK.** It must not disparage legally permitted ingredients ("paraben-free" is the classic unacceptable example), must not imply a benefit from simply complying with the law, and must be true for the function too. "Fragrance-free" means no ingredient with a perfuming function, including masking fragrance.
  - Prefer "formulated without X" plus a neutral reason ("because some sensitive skin reacts to fragrance").
  - US practice is looser, but stay consistent with the EU-safe wording.
- **"Fragrance-free" versus "unscented".** "Unscented" can still contain masking fragrance. A sensitive-skin brand should say fragrance-free and mean it.

### 7.7 Environmental claims

- **EU.** The Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive (2024/825) has applied since **27 September 2026**.
  - It bans generic environmental claims ("eco", "green", "sustainable", "climate-friendly") unless backed by recognised excellent environmental performance.
  - It bans product claims of climate neutrality based on offsetting, and sustainability labels not based on a certification scheme or public authority.
  - It bans presenting a claim about one part (the packaging) as if it covered the whole product.
  - The separate Green Claims Directive is slated for withdrawal (Commission work programme, 4 Nov 2026), but the ban above already applies.
- **UK.** The CMA Green Claims Code. The CMA can now fine directly under the DMCC Act.
- **US.** FTC Green Guides. California's rules on recyclability claims.
- **Rule for Lumora:** only specific, verifiable statements. For example "Bottle: 50% post-consumer recycled glass. Pipette: not yet recyclable kerbside." In the trial, keep environmental copy minimal.

### 7.8 Reviews, testimonials and influencers

- **US.** The FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR 465) has applied since 21 Oct 2024.
  - It bans fake or AI-generated reviews, reviews misrepresenting experience, bought reviews (positive or negative), undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression, and fake social-influence indicators.
  - Civil penalties exceed $50,000 per violation ($51,744 in 2024, adjusted for inflation each year).
  - The Endorsement Guides (2023) require disclosure of material connections such as free product or payment.
- **UK.** The DMCC Act 2024 has applied since 6 April 2025. Fake reviews, concealed incentivised reviews and misleading presentation of reviews are banned outright.
  - Publishers must take "reasonable and proportionate steps" and have a review policy (CMA guidance CMA208).
  - Fines reach up to 10% of global turnover.
- **EU.** The Omnibus Directive requires traders to say whether and how they verify that reviews come from real purchasers or users, and bans fake reviews.
- **Trial rule.** Any review, testimonial or tester quote is visibly labelled as sample content. Never write a review as a real person.

### 7.9 Prices, subscriptions and urgency

- **EU price reductions.** Any reduction must show the lowest price of the previous 30 days.
- **EU unit pricing.** Show a unit price where required (per 100 ml).
- **US subscriptions.** The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025. ROSCA, Section 5 and state laws still apply, and California's amended Automatic Renewal Law (1 July 2025) requires express consent, online click-to-cancel and annual reminders.
- **EU subscriptions and dark patterns.** The Commission adopted the **Digital Fairness Act** proposal on **11 Nov 2026**. It targets dark patterns, including fake low-stock warnings, and subscription traps, with a termination button for online subscriptions. It is a proposal, not yet law, but it shows where the rules are heading.
- **Fake urgency** (invented scarcity, countdowns that reset) is already an unfair practice under the existing EU and UK regimes and FTC Section 5.

### 7.10 Accessibility

- **EU.** The European Accessibility Act has applied to consumer e-commerce since **28 June 2025**. WCAG 2.1 AA is the practical benchmark. Micro-enterprises (fewer than 10 staff and €2M or less) are exempt for services, but build to the standard anyway.
- **US.** ADA website lawsuits often target beauty brands.
- **Essentials:**
  - Body text contrast of 4.5:1, and 3:1 for large text and UI elements.
  - Alt text on every image, and captions on video.
  - Visible focus states and labelled form fields.
  - Don't rely on colour alone (for selected size, for example).
  - Respect reduced-motion settings for the marquee and other animations.
  - Tap targets of 44 px (the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum is 24 px).

### 7.11 Scope of the six dummy products

Keep the line cosmetic-only. **Do not include SPF** (an OTC drug in the US with Drug Facts labelling), acne treatments (benzoyl peroxide or salicylic acid acne claims are OTC drugs), hair-growth products or skin-lightening products. A cleanser, essence, serum, eye gel, moisturiser and lip treatment give a complete ritual with no drug-claim exposure. On ritual graphics, write "your daily SPF" as an unbranded step.

### 7.12 If Lumora launched for real (MoCRA and the rest)

- **MoCRA.** Facility registration and product listing, serious adverse event reporting, safety substantiation records, and a US contact on the label for adverse event reports.
  - The fragrance allergen labelling rule is still pending (the proposal keeps slipping).
  - The GMP rule is listed as a long-term action.
  - FDA withdrew its December 2024 talc testing proposal.
- **Other jurisdictions.** State ingredient bans (California, Washington, Minnesota and others, including PFAS) for "formulated without" lists. EU and UK: a Responsible Person, a safety report (CPSR), CPNP/SCPN notification and unit pricing.
- **Get a regulatory review of all claims before launch.**

## 8. What makes a Shopify beauty store look cheap

| Mistake | Why it reads cheap | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Inconsistent product imagery (angles, light, backgrounds, crops, shadows) | Signals a dropship assortment, not a brand line | One art-direction spec: same ground, light direction, camera height and 4:5 ratio for all six |
| AI image tells: garbled label text, warped pumps, impossible reflections, plastic skin, extra fingers | Expert reviewers spot them instantly | Blender renders with real label artwork; inspect every asset at 200% |
| Leftover theme defaults: demo copy, demo images, "Subscribe to our emails", default favicon, "Powered by Shopify", default 404, password page and cart empty state | Shows we didn't finish | Sweep every template, notification and state (section 10) |
| Too many fonts, default theme fonts, weak hierarchy, light weights at small sizes, long all-caps runs | Templated and hard to read | Two families, a defined type scale, regular-weight body at 15–16 px or more |
| Low-contrast beige-on-cream text, too many accent colours, neon gradients | Hard to read, and it looks unconsidered | Colour tokens, one accent, AA contrast |
| Uneven spacing and misaligned grid edges between sections | Tells the eye nobody checked | One spacing scale; check alignment at 1440, 1280, 390 and 360 |
| Card clutter: several badges, wishlist, compare, swatches and quick-view icons | Marketplace look | At most one badge; turn off compare and wishlist; a single quick add |
| Discount-led design: strikethrough prices, "SALE", "% OFF", spin-to-win | Cheapens a premium launch (and EU price rules apply) | No compare-at prices; any offer is one quiet line |
| Fake urgency: countdowns, "only 3 left", "23 viewing", purchase toasts | Reads as a dropship store and is increasingly illegal | Switch every one off (Minimog and FoxKit have them) |
| Badge soup: "100% secure" shields, gold guarantee seals, fake "As seen in" | Overcompensation signals distrust | A plain three-item trust row with our own icons |
| Generic AI copy ("Elevate your routine", "Unlock radiant skin"), emoji, exclamation marks | Sounds like every other store | Specific, sensory copy (section 6) |
| Text baked into images | Blurry on mobile, inaccessible, unsearchable | Live text over or next to images |
| Autoplaying multi-slide hero | Low engagement and visual noise | One hero |
| Mismatched icons (filled and outline, different stroke widths, emoji) | Feels pieced together | One custom SVG set, one stroke weight |
| Broken or empty states: "0 reviews" stars, empty review widgets, a broken Instagram feed, "Sold out" dummies, $0.00 prices, dead nav links | The single most damaging thing in a trial review | Anchor or remove links, untrack inventory, set real prices, use labelled sample reviews |
| App widgets in their own fonts and colours (yellow stars, blue buttons) | Breaks the design system | Restyle every app with CSS to brand tokens |
| Stacked overlays on mobile: cookie banner, chat bubble, pop-up, sticky bars | Covers the product and the button | One overlay at a time; delay pop-ups; position the chat bubble |
| Generic stock models, mismatched grading, a narrow range of skin tones | Off-brand and exclusionary | Curated Unsplash, graded to one look, with diverse casting |
| App-like cards with heavy shadows and inconsistent corner radius | Looks like a mobile app template, not beauty | Hairline borders, one radius (0–8 px) |
| Typos, inconsistent capitalisation, mixed units or spellings | Erodes credibility instantly | Style sheet (6.8) and a proofread pass |
| The same products repeated in several homepage carousels | Obvious padding with a six-product range | Each product appears at most twice |
| Vendor name or SKU above the title, default "Tax included…" lines | Theme defaults showing through | Hide them or rewrite them |
| Full-colour payment logo strips everywhere | Visual noise | Monochrome marks in the footer and once in the trust row |
| Slow pages with layout shift from late-loading apps | Feels broken, and conversion drops | Few apps, reserved space, optimised media (section 9) |
| A default-looking link preview when the URL is pasted into Slack | The client's first impression of the link | Set the social sharing image, page title and meta description |

## 9. Mobile specifics

**Context.** Mobile made up 56.4% of US online holiday sales in 2025 (Adobe), and beauty skews more mobile than average. The 390 px layout is the main design, not a shrunken desktop.

1. **Hero.** A dedicated 4:5 (or 9:16) art-directed crop, not a squeezed 16:9. Headline in two lines at most. CTA visible without scrolling. The next section peeks in.
2. **Announcement bar.** One line, static. The header is 56–64 px; it hides on scroll down and returns on scroll up.
3. **Menu drawer.** All six products as image rows, a ritual CTA and the guarantee line. Rows at least 48 px tall.
4. **Grids.** Two-column product grid, or a slider showing 1.3–1.5 cards. Visible quick-add buttons (no hover on touch). No three-column grids.
5. **Type.** H1 32–40 px, H2 26–32 px, body 15–16 px, small text 12–13 px at minimum. Line length 35–45 characters.
6. **Spacing.** 16–20 px side gutters and 56–64 px between sections. Use the same values everywhere.
7. **PDP gallery.** Full-bleed 4:5, swipe with dots or a counter and a peek of the next image. Tap opens full-screen zoom with pinch. Baymard found mobile users swipe almost universally, so make sure swipe works.
8. **PDP order.** Gallery, then title, rating and price, then benefits, then size, then purchase options, then Add to bag. Add to bag sits within about 1.5 screens of the top.
9. **Sticky add to bag.** Appears after the main button scrolls away. Thumb zone, safe-area inset, and never stacked with the bottom nav, chat bubble or cookie bar.
10. **Accordions** for long detail. Keep the three benefits and the how-to-use summary visible. Open one accordion at a time.
11. **Tables become cards.** The comparison and suitability matrices stack, or scroll sideways with a sticky first column.
12. **Footnotes** go directly under the statistic, in the same viewport, never only in the footer.
13. **Reviews.** Summary first, then horizontally scrolling filter chips (skin type first), a photo-review row, and "Load more" instead of infinite scroll.
14. **Pop-ups.** None on landing. Show the newsletter as a small bottom sheet after engagement (30–60 s or 50% scroll), with a dismiss target of 44 px or more. Google penalises intrusive interstitials.
15. **Tap targets** of 44 × 44 px with spacing between them. Variant pills and accordion rows meet this.
16. **Forms.** Inputs at 16 px or more (prevents iOS zoom), correct keyboard types (email, numeric), autofill on.
17. **Performance.**
    - Preload the hero image and keep it at about 200 KB or less at mobile size. Serve correctly sized images (Shopify's CDN handles format).
    - Use video only as short, compressed loops with a poster frame.
    - Keep apps few, lazy-load below the fold and reserve space for late widgets.
    - Targets: LCP under 2.5 s, INP under 200 ms, CLS under 0.1.
18. **Motion.** A slow marquee, no scroll-jacking or parallax, and respect for reduced-motion settings.
19. **Cart drawer.** Full height, with a progress bar, one upsell, express wallets and a large Checkout button.
20. **Test** at 360, 375, 390/393 and 430 px, in iOS Safari (check the bottom toolbar overlap) and Android Chrome.

## 10. Pre-ship checklist (reference parity)

**Content and claims**
- [ ] Every statistic has a numbered footnote directly beneath it and is marked "Illustrative placeholder data". The footer demo note is present.
- [ ] No red-list words (7.2) anywhere: copy, product names, alt text, metafields or SEO fields.
- [ ] No certification logos, press logos or award seals. Badges are plain text and plausible.
- [ ] Reviews, testimonials and tester quotes are labelled as sample content. No repeated sentences.

**Catalogue and states**
- [ ] All six products have realistic prices, consistent 4:5 imagery, metafields filled, inventory untracked and no compare-at prices.
- [ ] The signature PDP template is assigned to Halo Serum only, and the default template looks finished on the other five.
- [ ] Empty states written: empty bag, search with no results, 404, the newsletter success and error messages, and the password page (if the store is locked).

**Links and navigation**
- [ ] Every header, footer and button link resolves to an anchor, the built PDP, a polished default PDP or the lightly styled all-products collection.

**Design system**
- [ ] Two typefaces, one icon set, one corner radius, one spacing scale, AA contrast.
- [ ] Review, subscription and bundle apps restyled to brand.

**Mobile**
- [ ] Checked at 390 and 360: no horizontal scroll, the sticky add-to-bag bar doesn't collide with anything, tap targets are 44 px, footnotes are visible.

**Performance and accessibility**
- [ ] LCP image preloaded and video compressed.
- [ ] Lighthouse mobile run with no layout shift from apps.
- [ ] Alt text, focus states and reduced-motion handling in place.

**First impressions**
- [ ] Favicon, social sharing image, page titles and meta descriptions set (the Slack link preview is the client's first view).
- [ ] Checkout branded: logo, colours, fonts.

**Remove**
- [ ] Countdowns, live-view counters, stock counters, order pop-ups, spin wheels, "Powered by Shopify" and any leftover demo content.
