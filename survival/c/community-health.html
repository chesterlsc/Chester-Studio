#📊 What really kills a community

Public health is the work of protecting everyone at once. A doctor treats one person at a time. A community that guards its water, latrines, food and records protects everyone together, and none of it needs electricity.

The numbers below come from WHO data and real disasters. They point to the same short list: dirty water, human waste, dirty hands, crowding and missed care.

Water, waste and hands

Unsafe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) kill on a huge scale. A 2023 WHO report found that safe WASH could have prevented at least 1.4 million deaths in 2019. More than 1 million of them were from diarrhea.1

Hands alone matter. WHO linked about 384,000 diarrheal deaths and 356,000 deaths from respiratory infections in 2019 to poor hand hygiene.1

Young children pay the most. Diarrhea is the third leading cause of death in children aged 1–59 months. It kills about 443,832 children under 5 every year.2

Cholera is a current disease. In 2024, 60 countries reported 560,823 cholera cases and 6,028 deaths to WHO.12 Cholera deaths rose for a second year in a row, and by September 2025, 31 countries had reported outbreaks that year.25

Disasters: crowding and missed care

A review in the CDC journal Emerging Infectious Diseases studied outbreaks after natural disasters. Displacement was the main risk factor. Disasters that did not displace people rarely led to outbreaks.3

The same review tied outbreak risk to crowding, safe water and sanitation, nutrition, vaccination coverage and access to health care.3 A community can work on every one of these.

Missed care kills slowly. After Hurricane Maria in 2017, a household survey linked about one third of excess deaths to delayed or interrupted health care.4 Poor and elderly people had the highest risk of dying.5

#🚰 Water, latrines and soap: the first medicine

Plan water by the head count. The Sphere humanitarian standard sets a minimum of 15 liters (about 4 gallons) per person per day for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene.7

Sphere splits basic needs into 2.5–3 L for drinking and food, 2–6 L for hygiene and 3–6 L for cooking.7 How to find and treat that much water is in Disinfecting Water.

One protected water point

No hands, cups or dippers in the water. CDC guidance says wide-mouth containers let hands, cups and dippers carry germs into treated water. Safe containers have a small opening, a lid and a tap.23

Big batches need measured bleach. The EPA dose is 2/3 teaspoon of 6% unscented bleach per 8 gallons (30 L) of clear water. Double it for cloudy, colored or very cold water, then wait 30 minutes.22

Chlorine does not stop every germ. CDC rates chlorine and iodine as not effective against Cryptosporidium, and boiling as very effective.21 Boil drinking water for babies and the sick whenever you have fuel.

Latrines and handwashing

Build enough latrines. UNHCR's target is 1 latrine per 20 people, or 1 per household. In the first phase of an emergency, no latrine should serve more than 50 people.6

Put soap and water beside every latrine. The Sphere standards call for handwashing facilities close to latrines.6 Building them is covered in Toilets Without Water.

Keep waste away from water. Diarrheal outbreaks after disasters can follow contamination of drinking water.3 Site latrines and garbage pits well away from the water point and the kitchen.

⏳ The 30-year view

Bleach is a first-year tool

EPA's method uses bleach stored for less than one year.22 At about 100 mL a day, 60 people would use about 36 L (9.5 gallons) of 6% bleach a year. Plan boiling, filters and pool shock for the decades after. See Disinfecting Water.

🐕 Dogs & pets

Dogs and the water point

Keep dogs out of latrines, the kitchen, the water point and the sick room. CDC says to store extra water for pets, so count every dog in the water math.19 Rabies and other animal diseases are in Rabies & Diseases Animals Give Humans.

#🧑‍⚕️ Train your own community health helpers

Pick helpers, not heroes. A health helper is a trusted neighbor trained to spot danger signs, start simple care, keep records and send people on to a clinic whenever one works.

Teach a short list well. Drill the danger signs in the table below until every helper can recite them and act on them without looking.

Step by step · 6 steps

  1. Choose at least two helpers

    Pick calm adults the community trusts, who can read and count. Two means one can rest or fall sick.

  2. Include a woman

    Women and girls can then choose a woman to talk to about periods, pregnancy and assault.

  3. Teach the danger signs

    Use the table below. Practice counting a child's breaths for a full minute.

  4. Teach a few safe actions

    ORS and continued feeding for diarrhea,8 CPR and bleeding control. Get formal courses while teachers exist.

  5. Give them tools

    A watch with a second hand, a thermometer, ORS sachets, soap, gloves, the tally sheet and the register.

  6. Meet every week

    Review each sick person, the tally sheet and the medicine count. Write down what you decided.

⚠️ Caution

Helpers do not prescribe

Health helpers never hand out prescription drugs on their own, and never use veterinary, fish or aquarium antibiotics. Those decisions belong to a licensed prescriber. See Infections, Sepsis & Antibiotic Facts.

🎯 Practice now

Practice while teachers exist

Book every helper into a first aid and CPR course now. Once a year, count each child's breathing at rest for a full minute and write it in the register. A sick child's count means more when you know their healthy one.

🌸 Women & girls

Women as helpers

Train at least one woman as a helper and one as a birth assistant. Women's specific health needs are covered in Pregnancy Care Without a Hospital and the chapters that follow it.

#📋 Paper surveillance: count it, write it, compare it

Surveillance means counting the same things, the same way, every week. When one count jumps above your normal, that is your early warning. Paper and a pencil are enough.

Use fixed case definitions. WHO defines diarrhea as 3 or more loose or liquid stools in a day, and acute bloody diarrhea as dysentery.2 If every helper counts the same way, the numbers can be compared.

Cholera's typical sign is milky "rice-water" diarrhea, often with vomiting and leg cramps.10 Hepatitis A can cause yellow skin or eyes, dark urine and clay-colored stools.14

Step by step · 6 steps

  1. Count new cases only

    Mark each sick person once, on the day the sign starts. A child sick for 5 days is 1 mark, not 5.

  2. Write one line per case

    The line list holds the details the tally sheet cannot (see the table below).

  3. Add every birth and death

    No exceptions. These two rows matter most over 30 years.

  4. Send even the zeros

    A sheet of zeros proves someone looked. A missing sheet tells you nothing.

  5. Compare with past weeks

    After a few weeks you know your normal. A clear jump starts the outbreak loop below.

  6. Keep every sheet dry

    Store them in a sealed container. See Preserving Knowledge & Records.

Count every birth and every death

Death counts are easy to get wrong. After Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico's first official death count was 64. A household survey estimated 4,645 excess deaths to the end of 2017.4

A study commissioned by the governor estimated 2,975 excess deaths from September 2017 to February 2018.5 Excess deaths are deaths above the number normally expected.5 You can only see them if you have been counting.

For each death, write the name, age, sex, date, household and the signs before death in plain words. For each birth, write the date, the mother, and whether the baby breathed and fed well.

🧓 Elderly & disabled

Older adults on the register

After Maria, poor and elderly people had the highest risk of dying, and older men had a 35% higher risk of death.5 Put every older adult on the health register with their medicines and the neighbor who checks on them. See Caring for Elderly & Disabled People.

🎯 Practice now

Run a practice month

Tally your own household's diarrhea, coughs and fevers for 4 weeks, starting now. You learn the form while mistakes cost nothing.

#🤰 Mothers, babies and young children first

Protect the people with the least reserve. CDC lists children under 5, pregnant people and adults 65 and older among those more likely to get seriously ill from food poisoning.17

Feed them first. FEMA and the Red Cross say healthy people can survive on half their usual food for an extended period. Food should not be rationed for children and pregnant women.18

Pregnant and breastfeeding women need more protein: 71 g a day in the US reference tables.20 CDC also says to store extra water for pregnant women.19

🌸 Women & girls

Pregnancy and water treatment

CDC does not recommend iodine-treated water during pregnancy.21 Give pregnant women boiled water, or water treated with bleach. Birth planning is in Emergency Childbirth: Step by Step.

The child health basics

Diarrhea: ORS, zinc and keep feeding. For children with diarrhea, WHO and UNICEF recommend low-osmolarity ORS, zinc supplements and continued feeding.8 Zinc shortens an episode of diarrhea by 25%.2

Take the zinc dose from a clinician or the package label. The full ORS method is in Diarrhea, Dehydration & Oral Rehydration.

Oral rehydration calculator. Interactive in the digital edition.

Healthy-looking children can spread germs. About 70% of hepatitis A infections in children under 6 cause no symptoms.13 Wash hands with soap after every diaper change.

Vaccination records are health records. Measles risk after disasters depends on how many people were already vaccinated, especially children under 15.3 Hepatitis A is also vaccine-preventable.13 See Vaccine-Preventable Diseases to Recognize.

The mother-and-child register

  • Every pregnant woman: due date, who will attend the birth, and the nearest working clinic
  • Every baby under 1: birth date, feeding (breast or other), weight checks, illnesses
  • Every child under 5: vaccination record and every episode of diarrhea or fast breathing
  • Every new mother: date of birth and any warning signs (see After Birth: Bleeding, Recovery & Warning Signs)
👶 Babies & children

Babies and diarrhea

Diarrhea is the third leading cause of death in children aged 1–59 months.2 In 2019, unsafe WASH caused 395,000 deaths of children under 5, including 273,000 from diarrhea.1 Babies keep breastfeeding through diarrhea.8 See Breastfeeding: The Survival Superpower.

#🚧 Outbreak plan: isolation, quarantine and response

Write the plan before the first case. Decide now where the sick will stay, who will care for them, and who tells everyone what to do.

Isolation separates people who are sick. Quarantine separates well people who were exposed, until the time when symptoms could start has passed. Infection control in the sick room is in Epidemics: Quarantine, Isolation & Infection Control.

Step by step · 7 steps

  1. Spot it

    One row of the tally sheet is clearly higher than your normal week.

  2. Check it

    Visit each family. Do the cases really match the case definition?

  3. Separate and treat

    Move the sick to the isolation room. For watery diarrhea, start ORS at once: cholera can kill within hours.11

  4. Find the source

    Count back from each first symptom with the table below. Look for a shared water source, meal or latrine.

  5. Block the route

    Close or treat the suspect water, fix latrines, add soap. Keep the sick out of food preparation.

  6. Tell everyone

    One short message from trusted people: what to watch for, what to do, where to bring the sick.

  7. Watch the window

    Keep tallying. New cases can still appear up to the longest incubation time after the last exposure.

DiseaseSymptoms start after exposureLongest wait
CholeraA few hours to 5 days, usually 2–3105 days

Do the arithmetic. If the last exposure to a hepatitis A case was on day 10, a new case can still start as late as day 60 (10 plus 50).13 For cholera, that window closes on day 15 (10 plus 5).10

☠️ Danger

Cholera can kill within hours

WHO says untreated cholera can kill within hours, and most cases can be treated with ORS.11 About 1 in 10 people get severe symptoms. With early, proper treatment, even people with severe cholera can survive.10 Start ORS at the first watery stools.

Setting up the isolation room

Do

  • Pick a room or tent away from the kitchen and water point
  • Name one carer per patient, and a backup
  • Put soap and water at the door
  • Give the patient their own cup, plate and toilet bucket
  • Write every patient on the line list

Don't

  • Let a person who had typhoid cook for others without a clinician's advice15
  • Let children visit or play near the room
  • Share cups, towels or bedding
  • Empty waste near the water point

The carer is the person most exposed. Hepatitis A spreads by close contact,13 and people who had typhoid can keep carrying and spreading it after their symptoms are gone.15 Care at home is in Nursing the Sick at Home.

Dead bodies left after a disaster start epidemics.

A 2007 review found no evidence that dead bodies cause epidemics. Deaths in rapid-onset disasters come mostly from blunt trauma, crush injuries and drowning.3 Still handle the dead with care and hygiene: see Death, Dying & Handling the Dead Safely.

If a child looks healthy, they cannot spread hepatitis A.

About 70% of hepatitis A infections in children under 6 cause no symptoms.13 Wash hands after every diaper change and every latrine visit.

💡 Tip

Coughs too

Hand hygiene is not only about diarrhea. WHO linked 356,000 deaths from respiratory infections in 2019 to poor hand hygiene.1 Put soap at the isolation room door for every kind of outbreak. See Handwashing & Personal Hygiene.

#💊 Medicines, chronic illness and records for 30 years

Missed care was a major killer after Maria. About one third of excess deaths were linked to delayed or interrupted health care.4 A community that knows who needs which medicine can act before supplies run out.

Keep one health register. For each person write: medicines and days of supply left, long-term conditions, allergies, devices that need power, vaccination record and any pregnancy due date. Update it at the weekly meeting.

Use the WHO Essential Medicines List as a planning map. The 2025 edition, published on 5 September 2025, lists 523 medicines for adults and 374 for children.24 Talk it through with a doctor or pharmacist while you can. See Building a Long-Term Medical Kit.

    Map the water point, latrines and isolation room. Start the health register.

  2. Month 1

    Two helpers trained in the danger signs. Weekly tally sheets start, zeros included.

  3. Year 1

    You know your normal week. Bleach bought before the outage is now over one year old.22

  4. Year 5+

    Helpers train new helpers. Birth and death registers show whether children are surviving.

⏳ The 30-year view

The 30-year view

Supplies run out; skills and records do not. Over decades, the jobs that last are the cheap ones: a protected water point, enough latrines, soap, a trained helper, and a notebook that counts births and deaths. Teach them to teenagers. See Passing On Skills to the Next Generation.

🛒 Get it before you need it

Get this before you need it

  • Low-osmolarity ORS sachets8 and soap in bulk
  • Bound notebooks, pencils, sharpeners and waterproof bags
  • A watch with a second hand and a thermometer for each helper
  • Food-grade water containers with taps23
  • Gloves, buckets with lids and materials for latrines

#🧠 Remember this

What is diarrhea, by WHO's definition?

3 or more loose or liquid stools in a day. Bloody diarrhea is dysentery.

How many people per shared latrine?

Target 1 per 20 people, or 1 per household. Never more than 50 in the first phase.

Minimum water per person per day?

15 L (about 4 gal) for drinking, cooking and hygiene (Sphere).

Fast breathing in a child aged 1 to 5?

40 or more breaths a minute. 50 or more at 2–11 months.

Whose food is never rationed?

Children and pregnant women (FEMA and Red Cross).

How long can hepatitis A take to show?

15–50 days, 28 on average. Watch contacts that long.

What share of Maria's excess deaths came from missed care?

About one third.

Do dead bodies cause epidemics?

A 2007 review found no evidence. Still handle them safely.
