💡 Tip

Shortcuts you can do in your head

  • kg × 2.2 = lb. lb ÷ 2.2 = kg.
  • 1 liter is a little more than 1 US quart (about 6% more).
  • 1 meter is about 3.3 feet. 5 miles is about 8 km.
  • 1 mL is 1 cubic centimeter (cc). 1,000 mL is 1 liter.
⚠️ Caution

Check which gallon

A US gallon is 3.785 L. A UK (imperial) gallon is 4.546 L, about 20% bigger. If a book or label says "imperial" or "UK" gallon, use the bigger number.

Water is its own scale. One liter of water weighs 1 kilogram, and one US gallon weighs 8.33 pounds.1 For water, liters and kilograms are the same number.

#🌡️ Temperature: one formula, five numbers

To get °F, multiply °C by 1.8 and add 32. To get °C, subtract 32 from °F and divide by 1.8. That is the whole method.

Step by step · 3 steps

  1. °C to °F: × 1.8, then + 32

    Example: 70°C × 1.8 = 126. Add 32 and you get 158°F.

  2. °F to °C: − 32, then ÷ 1.8

    Example: 40°F − 32 = 8. Divide by 1.8 and you get 4.4°C.

  3. Write both on your labels

    Your thermometer, your book and your medicine box may use different scales.

Keep food at 40°F (4°C) or colder, or 140°F (60°C) or hotter. Bacteria grow fastest between those two numbers.17 A cooler must stay at 40°F (4°C) or below.18

Insulin vials and cartridges can stay out of the fridge for up to 28 days at 59–86°F (15–30°C). Pens and other products differ, so read your own label.14

"Double it and add 30" converts °C to °F well enough.

It is close near room temperature but wrong when it matters. At 70°C, the shortcut gives 170°F; the real answer is 158°F. That is the CDC's formula-water temperature.21 Use × 1.8 + 32.

👶 Babies & children

Formula water and boiling

CDC advises boiling water for formula for 1 minute, and using water of about 70°C (158°F) for young or vulnerable babies.21 Above 6,500 ft (about 2,000 m), boil water for 3 minutes.20 See Feeding Babies When Formula Runs Out.

#🪣 How much water is in that container?

Volume = area of the bottom × depth of the water. It works for any container with straight sides: drums, tanks, buckets, troughs.

Step by step · 5 steps

  1. Measure the inside

    Use centimeters or inches. Measure inside the walls, not outside.

  2. Find the bottom area

    Box: length × width. Round tank: 0.785 × diameter × diameter.

  3. Multiply by the water depth

    Use the depth of the water, not the height of the tank, to see what is left.

  4. Convert to liters or gallons

    • cubic cm ÷ 1,000 = liters
    • cubic inches ÷ 231 = US gallons
    • cubic feet × 7.48 = US gallons

  5. Write it on the container

    Liters when full, liters per cm of depth, and the full weight.

Example, round drum: 60 cm across, 80 cm of water. 0.785 × 60 × 60 × 80 = 226,080 cubic cm, so 226 L (60 gal). Each 1 cm of depth holds 2.8 L.

Example, box tank: 100 × 80 × 50 cm = 400,000 cubic cm, so 400 L (106 gal). It weighs about 400 kg (880 lb) when full, because 1 L of water weighs 1 kg.1

☠️ Danger

Full containers are heavy

A full 208 L (55 gal) drum weighs about 208 kg (458 lb).1 University of Nebraska Extension advises sturdy shelving for stored water because of its weight.3 Fill big containers where they will stay.

Leave 5–8 cm (2–3 inches) of space at the top of any container that might freeze.3

👶 Babies & children

Every open container is a drowning risk

A child can drown in less than 1 inch (2.5 cm) of water. Never leave water containers open and unattended.24 Keep lids on buckets and tanks.

🐕 Dogs & pets

Count your dogs in the water math

A pet kit needs a water bowl and several days' supply of water.25 CDC says to store extra water for pets.2 Add a line for each dog in your table, and measure what each one drinks in a day.

#🌧️ Roof catchment math

1 mm of rain on 1 m² of roof gives 1 liter. The reason: 1 mm × 1 m² = 0.001 cubic meters, which is exactly 1 liter.

In US units, 1 inch of rain on 1 square foot gives 0.62 gallon. 1 inch × 144 square inches = 144 cubic inches. A US gallon is 231 cubic inches. 144 ÷ 231 = 0.623.

Step by step · 4 steps

  1. Measure the footprint

    Length × width of the roof as seen from above, overhangs included. Slope does not add area.

  2. Multiply by the rainfall

    mm × m² = liters. Inches × sq ft × 0.623 = US gallons.

  3. Compare with your need

    4 people at 1 gallon a day need 1,460 gallons (5,527 L) a year.2

  4. Measure what you really catch

    After a measured rain, check how much the tank gained. Gained ÷ calculated = your roof's own ratio. Use it for future estimates.

Metric example: a 10 × 8 m footprint is 80 m². A 25 mm storm can give at most 2,000 L (528 gal). For 4 people at CDC's minimum, that roof must capture about 69 mm (2.7 in) of rain a year.2

US example: a 30 × 40 ft footprint is 1,200 sq ft. 1 inch of rain gives at most 748 gallons (2,831 L).

💡 Tip

Make a free rain gauge

Any container with straight, vertical sides measures rainfall. The depth of water in it equals the rain that fell, whatever its width. Check it at the same time each day and write the number down.

⏳ The 30-year view

The 30-year view: keep a rain log

One year of daily rain numbers shows your wettest and driest months. Size tanks to carry you through your longest dry stretch, not your average month. Treat roof water before drinking it: see Disinfecting Water. For gutters and first-flush diverters, see Rainwater Harvesting.

Bleach calculator. Interactive in the digital edition.

A few extra drops make clear water extra safe.

EPA gives a set dose per strength, and doubles it only for cloudy, colored or very cold water.5 Measure; do not pour by eye.

To sanitize a container, use 1 teaspoon (5 mL) of bleach per quart (about 1 L) of water. Shake it to coat every surface, wait at least 30 seconds, then pour it out.2

The dilution formula

Strength before × amount before = strength after × amount after. Written short: C1 × V1 = C2 × V2. It works for any dilution.

EPA's dry-chlorine method: 1 heaping teaspoon (about ¼ oz, 7 g) of high-test calcium hypochlorite in 2 gallons (7.6 L) of water makes a stock of about 500 mg/L. You then add 1 part stock to 100 parts water.6

Check it with the formula: 500 mg/L × 1 part ÷ 101 parts = about 5 mg/L. Because 1 L of water weighs 1 kg (1,000,000 mg), 1 mg/L is the same as 1 part per million (ppm).1

☠️ Danger

Stock solution is not drinking water

At about 500 mg/L, the stock is 100 times stronger than the treated water.6 Label it, cap it and keep it away from children. Never mix chlorine with other cleaners.

#🍚 Calorie and protein math

Plan 2,100 kcal per person per day. That is the minimum planning figure for humanitarian food rations.8 Adult women need about 1,600–2,400 kcal and men about 2,000–3,000, depending on age and activity.9

The rice column uses USDA's figure of about 365 kcal per 100 g of dry white rice, which is 3,650 kcal per kg.10 Rice is only an example. Calories are not a full diet: see Deficiency Diseases.

Step by step · 3 steps

  1. Find kcal per kg

    From the label: kcal per serving ÷ grams per serving × 1,000.

  2. Find the total kcal you hold

    kg of food × kcal per kg.

  3. Divide by the daily need

    Total kcal ÷ (people × kcal per day) = days of food.

Example: a 25 kg (55 lb) sack of white rice holds about 91,250 kcal.10 A family of 4 at 2,100 kcal each uses 8,400 kcal a day. 91,250 ÷ 8,400 = about 11 days.

Oil carries 2.4 times the calories of rice, kilo for kilo. Vegetable oil has about 884 kcal per 100 g; dry white rice has about 365.10 That matters when you carry food. See Calories & Nutrition.

Cutting everyone's food in half hurts everyone the same.

FEMA and the Red Cross say healthy people can survive on half their usual food intake for an extended period. Food may be rationed safely, except for children and pregnant women.7

Protein by body weight

Protein targets are grams per kilogram of body weight. The adult RDA is 0.8 g/kg.11 The Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2025–2030 set 1.2–1.6 g/kg a day.12

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Protein needs in pregnancy and breastfeeding are 1.1–1.3 g/kg a day.11 CDC says to store more water for pregnant women.2 Do not cut food for pregnant women or children when you ration.7

#💊 Dosing and loads by body weight

Weight-based math needs a real weight. Guessing a child's weight puts every later number wrong.

Step by step · 4 steps

  1. Weigh yourself

    Use a bathroom scale on a hard, flat floor.

  2. Weigh yourself holding the baby or dog

    Stand still and read the number.

  3. Subtract

    The difference is their weight. Convert: lb ÷ 2.2 = kg.

  4. Write it down with the date

    Children and dogs grow. Re-weigh before any weight-based dose.

Learn the unit ladder. 1 kg = 1,000 g. 1 g = 1,000 mg. 1 mg = 1,000 micrograms (mcg). 1 L = 1,000 mL. Mixing up mg and mcg is a 1,000-fold mistake.

Step by step · 5 steps

  1. Get the weight in kg

    Use the steps above.

  2. Find the dose on the label or from a clinician

    The dose comes from the label's chart or a written instruction, never from your own guess.

  3. Read the strength

    Labels give mg in a volume. "100 mg per 5 mL" means 20 mg in each mL.

  4. Measure in mL with the device that came with it

    Not a kitchen spoon.

  5. Write the time and amount

    Check the label's daily maximum before every dose.

Do

  • Weigh, then convert to kg
  • Measure liquids in mL
  • Use the device in the box
  • Log every dose and time

Don't

  • Guess a child's weight
  • Dose with kitchen spoons
  • Give aspirin to children
  • Work out a dog dose from a human one
☠️ Danger

Never give aspirin to children

Mayo Clinic's first-aid guidance is plain: never give aspirin to children.13 Use professional care whenever it exists. For what each medicine does, see Over-the-Counter Medicines.

🐕 Dogs & pets

Dogs are dosed by a vet, not by math

Do not convert a human dose for your dog. Weigh the dog with the hold-and-subtract method and give the weight to a veterinarian. See Dog Health.

Loads by body weight

A child's backpack should weigh 10–20% of the child's weight. An 80 lb (36 kg) child carries 8–16 lb (3.6–7.3 kg).15 For adults, a 2018 military study recommends loads of 30% of body mass or less.16

🌸 Women & girls

Loads scale with the body

At 30% of body mass, a 50 kg (110 lb) woman's limit is 15 kg (33 lb), and an 80 kg (176 lb) man's is 24 kg (53 lb).16 Split group loads by body weight, not evenly.

#⏱️ Estimating time: count it, don't guess

Count breaths and pulse for a full 60 seconds. WHO calls 50 or more breaths a minute fast breathing at 2–11 months, and 40 or more from 12 months to 5 years.22 See Assessing a Sick or Injured Person.

Calibrate your counting now

While you still have a watch, count "one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand" to 60 and check the clock. Repeat until you land within a few seconds. Then you have a timer that needs no battery.

  4. 1 hour

  5. 2 hours

A full closed freezer holds about 48 hours; a half-full one about 24 hours.19 Write down the time the power went off. See Food Safety Without a Refrigerator.

#🌳 Estimating distance and height

Your pace count

A pace count turns your legs into a tape measure. In this method, one pace is two steps: count each time the same foot lands.

Step by step · 5 steps

  1. Mark a measured 100 m

    Use a tape measure on flat ground. In feet, 100 m is 328 ft.

  2. Walk it normally and count

    Count every time your left foot lands.

  3. Repeat 3 times and average

    That average is your pace count per 100 m.

  4. Recalibrate for real conditions

    Walk the course again carrying your usual load, and on slopes, and keep a separate count for each.

  5. Use it

    Distance in meters = paces counted ÷ your pace count × 100.

Example: your count is 64 per 100 m and you counted 320 paces. 320 ÷ 64 × 100 = 500 m.

Use it for safety distances. CDC says to run a generator outdoors more than 20 feet (6.1 m) from windows, doors and vents.23 With a count of 64, 6.1 m is about 4 paces; pace it out and mark the spot.

Height from shadows

A stick and two shadows give a tree's height. At the same moment, every upright object casts a shadow in the same ratio to its height.

Step by step · 3 steps

  1. Stand a straight stick upright

    Measure the part above the ground.

  2. Measure both shadows

    The stick's shadow and the tree's shadow, base to tip, at the same time.

  3. Do the math

    Tree height = tree shadow × stick height ÷ stick shadow.

No sun? Use the arm's-length stick method. Hold a stick upright at arm's length, with the part above your fist as long as the distance from your eye to your fist. Walk until the stick's tip lines up with the treetop and your fist with the base. Your distance to the tree now equals its height; pace it.

💡 Tip

Know the reach before you fell

A tree that falls reaches as far as it is tall. Measure the height first, and keep people, dogs and roofs out of that circle.

#🌾 Area and land measures

Area is length × width for a rectangle. A triangle is base × height ÷ 2. A circle is 0.785 × diameter × diameter.

Step by step · 4 steps

  1. Pace or tape every side

    Sketch the plot on paper and write each length on it.

  2. Split the shape

    Cut odd shapes into rectangles and right-angle triangles.

  3. Work out each piece and add them

    Rectangles: length × width. Triangles: base × height ÷ 2.

  4. Convert

    m² ÷ 10,000 = hectares. m² ÷ 4,047 = acres. sq ft ÷ 43,560 = acres.

💡 Tip

Square corners with a 3-4-5 rope

Mark a rope at 3 m, 4 m and 5 m (or 3, 4 and 5 ft). Pull it into a triangle: the corner between the 3 and 4 sides is a perfect right angle, because 3² + 4² = 5². Use it for beds, sheds and fences.

Seed packets often give rates per 100 sq ft. 100 sq ft is 9.3 m², so "per 100 sq ft" is close to "per 10 m²". For yields and land per person, see Feeding a Family Forever.

⏳ The 30-year view

The 30-year view: keep your own standards

Tools wear out, but water does not change. A 1-liter bottle full of water weighs 1 kg, so it can check a homemade balance scale.1 Keep one tape-measured 1 m stick, and recheck each child's pace count and weight every year.

  20. CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency (2024). Boil 1 minute; 3 minutes above 6,500 feet.
  21. CDC: Prepare and Store Powdered Infant Formula in an Emergency (2024). Boil water 1 minute; about 70°C water for young or vulnerable infants.
  22. World Health Organization: Pneumonia policy brief on the revised classification and treatment of childhood pneumonia (2014). Fast breathing: 50 or more a minute at 2–11 months, 40 or more at 12 months to 5 years.
  23. CDC: Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics (2024). Run generators outdoors more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents; never in a home or garage.
  24. Nemours KidsHealth: Drowning Safety for Parents (n.d.). Drowning can happen in less than 1 inch of water; never leave water containers unattended.
  25. Ready.gov (FEMA): Prepare Your Pets for Disasters (n.d.). A pet kit includes a water bowl and several days' supply of water.
