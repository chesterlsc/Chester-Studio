#🔤 How to use this glossary

This is the book's dictionary. When a word or a set of capital letters stops you, look it up here, read one line, then follow the link.

Each table runs A to Z. Short forms (letters like ORS or MARCH) have their own section. So do the organizations behind the facts.

Numbers and safety rules carry a source. Plain word meanings link to the chapter that covers the topic in depth, with its own sources.

Step by step · 4 steps

  1. Spot the type of word

    All capital letters? Go to Short forms. An organization? Go to Who's who. Anything else: go to its first letter.

  2. Find the row

    Terms run A to Z inside each table. The first column is bold.

  3. Read the one-line meaning

    A number in the meaning is a rule. Learn it together with the word.

  4. Follow the chapter link

    The last column takes you to the full steps, warnings and sources.

☠️ Danger

Five terms that mean act now, read later

  • Cardiac arrest: no response and not breathing, or only gasping. Start CPR and use an AED.6
  • Choking: for an adult or child, alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts. For a baby under 1, use back blows and chest thrusts.4
  • Carbon monoxide: headache, dizziness, weakness, vomiting or confusion near burning fuel. Get to fresh air, then get medical help.2
  • Massive bleeding: in an injured person, MARCH puts it first.7 See Stopping Severe Bleeding.
  • Cholera: untreated, it can kill within hours. Start oral rehydration solution (ORS).17

#🌡️ Words that come with a number

Some words are rules with a number attached. Learn the number together with the word, because the number tells you what to do.

The thermometer below gathers the temperature words from across the book. The timeline after it gathers the time words.

Time words, from seconds to years

  1. 2 seconds

    Capillary refill: nail color should return in under this time.9

  2. 1 minute

    Rolling boil for clear water. Make it 3 minutes above 2,000 m (6,500 ft).10

  3. 20 minutes

    Bacteria in the Danger Zone can double this fast.13

  4. 30 minutes

    Wait this long after adding bleach before you drink.11

  5. 2 hours

    Perishable food above 4°C (40°F) for longer than this goes in the trash.14

  6. 4 hours

    A closed fridge keeps food safe this long without power.14

  7. 24 or 48 hours

    A half-full or full freezer, with the door shut.14

  8. 28 days

    Insulin vials and cartridges out of the fridge at 15–30°C (59–86°F).16

  9. 6 months

    Replace water you stored yourself.22

  10. 5 years

    Replace carbon monoxide alarms.1

Gasping means the person is still breathing.

Gasping can happen after the heart stops. Treat "only gasping" as not breathing and start CPR.36

Boiled water is safe from everything.

Boiling kills germs. It does not remove chemicals.15

Hands-Only CPR is right for everyone.

It is for teens and adults who suddenly collapse. Infants, children, and people who drowned, overdosed or had breathing problems need breaths too.5

👶 Babies & children

Baby words

In CPR and choking guidance, an infant is a baby under 1 year. A choking infant gets back blows and chest thrusts with the heel of one hand, never abdominal thrusts.4

For powdered formula for young or vulnerable babies, CDC uses water at about 70°C (158°F).15 More baby words are in Newborn Care.

🐕 Dogs & pets

Words that include your dog

CDC's carbon monoxide warning covers animals too: the gas builds up and poisons people and animals.1 CDC also says to store extra water for pets.22

A zoonosis is a disease that passes between animals and people, such as rabies. See Rabies & Zoonoses.

🌸 Women & girls

Women's and girls' health words

CDC says to store more water for pregnant women.22 Postpartum means the weeks after birth. A postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is heavy bleeding after birth.

Other terms you will meet: pre-eclampsia, mastitis, LAM and toxic shock syndrome (TSS). Start with Pregnancy Care and After Birth.

#🧠 Remember this

What does "only gasping" mean?

Not breathing. It is cardiac arrest: start CPR and use an AED.

What is the Danger Zone?

4–60°C (40–140°F). Bacteria can double in 20 minutes.

What does ORS stand for?

Oral rehydration solution: salts and sugar in clean water to replace fluid lost to diarrhea.

What does MARCH stand for?

Massive bleeding, Airway, Respiration, Circulation, Hypothermia and head injury. Bleeding comes first.

What does SAMPLE stand for?

Signs and symptoms, Allergies, Medicines, Past history, Last in and out, Events.

What is a normal capillary refill?

Nail color returns in under 2 seconds.

Which bleach goes in drinking water?

Plain unscented bleach, less than 1 year old.

How long can insulin vials stay out of the fridge?

Up to 28 days at 15–30°C (59–86°F). Pens differ.

  8. MedlinePlus (NIH): Vital signs (n.d.). Normal vital signs change with age, sex, weight, exercise capability and overall health; healthy adult blood pressure 90/60 to 120/80 mmHg.
  9. MedlinePlus (NIH): Capillary nail refill test (n.d.). Nail bed color should return in under 2 seconds after pressure is released.
  10. CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency (2024). Boil 1 minute, 3 minutes above 6,500 ft; settle or cloth-filter cloudy water; bleach and iodine less effective against Cryptosporidium and Giardia; chlorine dioxide can kill Cryptosporidium if instructions are followed.
  11. EPA: Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water (n.d.). Bottled water first; unscented bleach less than 1 year old; double for cloudy, colored or very cold water; 30-minute wait; aerate strong-tasting water.
  12. EPA: Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water (archived page) (2013, archived). High-test granular calcium hypochlorite, about 70% available chlorine; 1 heaping teaspoon in 2 gallons makes a stock of about 500 mg/L; mix 1 part stock to 100 parts water.
  13. USDA FSIS: Danger Zone (40°F–140°F) (n.d.). Bacteria grow fastest between 40°F and 140°F and can double in as little as 20 minutes.
  14. USDA FSIS: Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies (n.d.). Fridge 4 hours; full freezer 48 hours, half full 24; 40°F and 0°F targets; 50 lb of dry ice holds a full 18 cu ft freezer 2 days; do not touch dry ice; refreezing; discard perishables above 40°F for 2 hours.
  15. CDC: Prepare and Store Powdered Infant Formula in an Emergency (2024). Water at about 70°C for young or vulnerable infants; boiling does not remove chemicals.
  16. FDA: Information Regarding Insulin Storage and Switching Between Products in an Emergency (n.d.). Vials and cartridges, opened or unopened, may stay unrefrigerated at 59–86°F for up to 28 days; pens differ.
  17. WHO: Cholera fact sheet (n.d.). Cholera can kill within hours if untreated; most cases can be treated with ORS.
  18. WHO/UNICEF: Clinical management of acute diarrhoea, joint statement (2004). Low-osmolarity ORS and zinc supplements for children with diarrhea, with continued feeding.
  19. WHO: WHO updates list of essential medicines to include key cancer, diabetes treatments (2025). 24th adult list and 10th children's list; 523 adult and 374 children's medicines.
  20. New England Journal of Medicine: Mortality in Puerto Rico after Hurricane Maria (Kishore et al.) (2018). Household survey; about one third of deaths linked to delayed or interrupted medical care.
  21. NASA: What NASA Is Learning From the Biggest Geomagnetic Storm in 20 Years (2025). May 2024 G5 storm, the first in over two decades; GPS-guided tractors thrown off course.
  22. CDC: How to Create an Emergency Water Supply (2024). At least 1 gallon per person per day, 2 weeks if possible; more for pregnant women, sick people, pets and hot climates; food-grade containers; replace every 6 months.
  23. Sphere Project: Water supply standard 1, access and water quantity (2011 edition). Minimum 15 L per person per day for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene.
  24. Global Food Security Cluster Handbook: 5.6.1 Food Assistance (n.d.). 2,100 kcal per person per day is the minimum planning figure for general food rations.
  25. CDC: What to Do in Radiation Emergencies (n.d.). Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned; go to the middle of the building or a basement.
