#🏝️ Puerto Rico after Maria: 11 months to reconnect Year 1

What happened

Hurricane Maria hit Puerto Rico on September 20, 2017.2 About 11 months later, officials said every part of the island had power again.1

A Harvard-led team surveyed 3,299 randomly chosen households. From landfall to December 31, 2017, homes averaged 84 days without electricity, 68 days without water and 41 days without cell phone service (NEJM, 2018).2

Remote homes waited longest. In remote areas, 83% of homes had no electricity for the whole period studied.2

How many people died

The first official death toll was 64. The household survey estimated 4,645 excess deaths from landfall to the end of 2017.2

A George Washington University (GWU) study, commissioned by Puerto Rico's governor, estimated 2,975 excess deaths from September 2017 to February 2018.7 "Excess deaths" are deaths above the number normally expected.

Missed care killed people for months. The survey linked about one third of excess deaths to delayed or interrupted medical care.2 The GWU study tracked excess deaths through February 2018, about five months after landfall.7

The GWU team found that poor and elderly people had the highest risk of dying.7

🧓 Elderly & disabled

Older adults carried the most risk

After Maria, older men had a death risk 35% higher than expected.7 Put the medicines, devices, warmth and cooling of older relatives at the top of your plan. See Caring for Elderly & Disabled People.

⚠️ Caution

First counts can be far too low

The first Maria count was 64.2 The study commissioned by the governor found 2,975, about 46 times more.7 Texas raised its 2021 count from 210 to 246.3 Always check the date on a number.

⏳ The 30-year view

The 30-year view

Puerto Rico's island-wide restoration took about 11 months.1 Thirty years is about 33 times longer (360 months ÷ 11), and this book assumes no repair crews arrive. Plan the long haul with Year 1 to Year 30: A Phased Plan.

#❄️ Texas, February 2021: up to four days in the cold First 72 h

More than 4.5 million people in Texas lost power, some for up to 4 days. Federal regulators found that frozen generating equipment was the main cause.8

Texas linked 246 deaths to the storm. The count rose from 210 in an earlier report. About two-thirds of the deaths were from cold exposure, mostly hypothermia, which means body temperature falling dangerously low.3

Water failed at the same time. Water system disruptions hit more than 14 million Texans.4

The lesson: a blackout of a few days can kill when it is cold. Most of the Texas deaths came from cold, not from the dark.3 Heat for the most vulnerable person in the house comes first. See Winter Storms & Extreme Cold.

👶 Babies & children

Babies and young children

Ready.gov warns that older adults and young children are especially vulnerable to extreme temperatures. If your home gets too cold or too hot, go to a place with heat or cooling.17 Plan the warmest spot in your home for the youngest child first: see Staying Warm Without Heat.

☠️ Danger

Never heat your home with a gas stove or oven

Ready.gov says never to use a gas stove or oven to heat your home.17 Read Carbon Monoxide: The Blackout Killer before winter comes.

#🚰 When the pumps stop, the water stops First 72 h

Tap water runs on electricity. Water and wastewater plants often use about 30–40% of a town's energy (EPA, 2024).18 Losing power for more than 30 minutes can disrupt wastewater treatment.18

Cleveland and Detroit, August 2003

On August 14, 2003, about 50 million people in the US and Canada lost power. The outage cut 61,800 megawatts of load across eight US states and Ontario.9

In Cleveland, the four major water supply pumps lost power. Taps gave only a trickle.10 Residents were then warned to boil their water.19

In Detroit, a boil-water alert covered 4.3 million customers. It was not lifted until August 19, five days after the blackout began.119

Asheville, North Carolina, 2024

After Hurricane Helene, Asheville did not lift its boil water notice for all customers until November 18, 2024.16 Local public radio called it a 53-day water crisis. The notice was lifted after 120 water samples were tested.15

Why it matters for health

A review in a CDC journal found that diarrheal outbreaks after disasters can follow contamination of drinking water.20 Keep toilet waste far away from anything you drink. See Toilets Without Water.

Step by step · 4 steps

  1. Fill clean containers at once

    Cleveland's taps dropped to a trickle when its pumps lost power.10 Fill every clean, lidded container while pressure lasts.

  2. Separate drinking water from washing water

    Label them. Keep drinking water covered and away from flushing and cleaning water.

  3. Treat tap water when it comes back

    Boil-water warnings followed the 2003 blackout in Cleveland and Detroit.1911 Boil or disinfect tap water until officials say it is safe. See Disinfecting Water.

  4. Count your days of water

    Asheville's water crisis lasted 53 days.15 Write down how many days your stored water covers each person.

When the water comes back on, it is safe to drink.

Not always. Cleveland residents were warned to boil water after the 2003 blackout.19 Asheville's boil notice ran until November 18, 2024.16 Treat tap water until officials say it is safe.

#🏭 Ukraine, 2022 to 2025: a grid under repeated attack Long haul

Before 2022, Ukraine had about 38 gigawatts (GW) of dispatchable power capacity.6 Dispatchable plants are the ones operators can turn up or down on demand.

Ukraine lost 19 GW to occupation, destruction and damage in the first year of the full-scale war. After more attacks in spring 2024, about 12 GW was left (IEA, 2025).6

That is about two-thirds of the capacity gone (38 − 12 = 26 GW; 26 ÷ 38 ≈ 68%). The attacks continued. On September 7–8, 2025, Russia carried out its largest air attack on Ukraine since February 2022, with major damage to energy infrastructure.6

The lesson: repeated damage kept capacity far below its pre-war level from 2022 into 2024, and major attacks were still hitting energy infrastructure in 2025.6

⏳ The 30-year view

The 30-year view

This book plans for a grid with no repairs at all. Build household systems that need no grid: see Low-Tech Power for the Long Haul and What Fails When: The 30-Year Timeline.

#🏧 Spain and Portugal, 2025: 16 hours without cash or trains

On April 28, 2025, a widespread blackout hit Spain and Portugal. Subway networks, traffic lights and ATMs stopped.13

Full restoration took about 16 hours. The expert panel's final report (2026) found no single trigger, with voltage management at the center of the failure.12

Even a 16-hour blackout was linked to deaths. Early reports linked at least three deaths to it. In Galicia, police investigated carbon monoxide (CO) from a generator after an older couple and their adult son died at home.14

Money and fuel stop first

Payments and fuel need power. FEMA lists fuel pumps, lighting and payment systems among the systems hit by power outages (2025).21 Ready.gov warns that ATMs and card machines may not work, and says to keep cash in your kit.17

Plan for a world without cards. See Barter, Trade & Money After Collapse.

☠️ Danger

"Outside" is not far enough for a generator

After Hurricanes Katrina and Rita in 2005, portable generators poisoned at least 78 people with CO and killed 10. Most of those generators were outside, but near the home.5

Keep generators outdoors and at least 6 m (20 ft) from windows. Fit CO alarms with battery backup.17 See Generators & Fuel Storage.

#🩺 What studies measured: how long blackouts hurt health

The studies and official counts behind these cases point to five killers. Each one is documented:

  • Missed medical care: about one third of Maria's excess deaths.2
  • Cold: about two-thirds of the 246 Texas deaths in 2021.3
  • Generator fumes: 10 deaths and at least 78 poisonings after Katrina and Rita.5
  • Unsafe water: diarrheal outbreaks can follow contamination of drinking water.20
  • Heat during evacuation: overheating in vehicles during the Hurricane Rita evacuation.22

Leaving can kill too

Nearly 3.7 million people left the Texas coast in the 4 days before Hurricane Rita in 2005. Over 100 of at least 119 deaths linked to Rita came from the evacuation itself.22

At least 10% of those evacuation deaths were linked to overheating in vehicles. A bus carrying nursing-home residents caught fire in the evacuation traffic, and about two dozen people died.22 Weigh both risks in Stay or Go?

People who need power to stay alive

In the US, more than 3 million Medicare patients have claims for electricity-dependent medical equipment. More than 4.6 million rely on that equipment or on services such as dialysis, oxygen or home health care (HHS emPOWER).23

Ready.gov says to make a plan for any medical device that needs power, and for medicines that must stay cold.17 It also says to discard refrigerated medicine after an outage of more than a day, unless the label says otherwise.17

⚠️ Caution

Do not throw away life-saving medicine

The one-day rule has an exception. If a life depends on a refrigerated medicine, Ready.gov says to ask a doctor or pharmacist and to use it only until a new supply is available.17 Insulin vials and cartridges can stay at 15–30°C (59–86°F) for up to 28 days.25

Displacement drives outbreaks

A review in CDC's journal Emerging Infectious Diseases linked disease outbreaks after disasters mainly to displacement. Disasters that did not displace people rarely led to outbreaks.20

The same review linked outbreak risk to crowding, water and sanitation, nutrition, vaccine immunity and access to health care.20 A safe home with clean water and a working toilet covers several of those.

A blackout kills in the first day or two. After that, it is just an inconvenience.

After Maria, excess deaths were counted from September 2017 to February 2018.7 About one third were linked to delayed or interrupted medical care.2

🌸 Women & girls

Women and girls

Plan period supplies, pregnancy care and a birth plan for months without power, not days. Puerto Rico homes averaged 84 days without electricity and 68 without water.2 See Periods Without Stores and Pregnancy Care Without a Hospital.

#⏳ How long restoration took

Restoration ranged from hours to years. Here are the documented times, shortest first.

  1. 16 hours

    Spain and Portugal, April 2025: full restoration.12

  2. Up to 4 days

    Texas, February 2021: more than 4.5 million people without power.8 Parts of the US also waited 4 days in August 2003.9

  3. 5 days

    Detroit, August 2003: boil-water alert for 4.3 million customers, lifted August 19.11

  4. Over 1 week

    Ontario, 2003: rolling blackouts in parts of the province.9

  5. 41 days

    Puerto Rico, 2017: average home without cell phone service.2

  6. 53 days

    Asheville, NC, 2024: water crisis after Hurricane Helene.15

  7. 68 days

    Puerto Rico, 2017: average home without water.2

  8. 84 days

    Puerto Rico, 2017: average home without electricity, counted to December 31.2

  9. About 11 months

    Puerto Rico: power restored to the whole island in 2018.1

  10. 2022 to 2025

    Ukraine: about 12 GW of 38 GW left after spring 2024; the largest air attack of the war came on September 7–8, 2025.6

In Puerto Rico, phones and water came back before power, on average. Homes averaged 41 days without cell service and 68 without water, against 84 without electricity.2

📊 Key fact

Even the longest repair was short next to 30 years

Puerto Rico's whole-island restoration took about 11 months.1 That is about 3% of 30 years (11 ÷ 360 months). Every system in your plan has to last far longer than any outage on record.

#🧭 Lessons for a 30-year outage Long haul

Each lesson below comes straight from the cases above. Each one turns a documented failure into an action.

1. Plan in months, then years

The Red Cross advises a 2-week supply at home.24 Puerto Rico homes averaged 84 days without power.2 Two weeks covers one sixth of that average. Start there, then keep building.

2. Medicine runs out first

The Red Cross kit list says a 7-day supply of medications.24 The FDA says insulin vials and cartridges can stay at 15–30°C (59–86°F) for up to 28 days; pens differ, so read the label.25 The average Maria outage was 3 times longer than 28 days.2

Talk to your doctor now about each daily medicine. See Chronic Conditions Without Pharmacies.

3. Water before everything

Cleveland's taps dropped to a trickle when the pumps lost power.10 Water problems hit more than 14 million Texans.4 Store water and know your nearest natural source: see Finding Water When the Taps Stop.

4. Warmth and cooling are life support

Cold caused about two-thirds of the Texas deaths.3 Overheating in vehicles killed Rita evacuees.22 Pick one warm room and one cool room now.

5. Fumes kill people who are trying to cope

Generator CO killed people after Katrina and Rita, and police investigated it after deaths in Spain.514 Most 2005 poisonings came from generators outside but near the home.5

6. Cash, paper and neighbors

ATMs stopped in Spain and Portugal.13 Ready.gov says to keep cash, use flashlights instead of candles, and check on your neighbors.17

Do

  • Plan for at least 84 days, then for years
  • Fill water containers the moment the power fails
  • Treat tap water until officials say it is safe
  • Keep a generator outdoors, at least 6 m (20 ft) from windows
  • Keep cash in small notes
  • Check on older and disabled neighbors

Don't

  • Trust the first death toll or the first repair estimate
  • Run a generator in a garage or near a window
  • Heat your home with a gas stove or oven
  • Drink tap water the moment pressure returns
  • Sit in hot evacuation traffic without water and a plan to stay cool
  • Count on card payments working

Test your household against the record

Step by step · 7 steps

  1. List every powered medical device

    Oxygen, dialysis, powered wheelchairs, home health equipment. More than 4.6 million US Medicare patients rely on such equipment or services.23 Make an outage plan for each one.17

  2. Count medicine days

    The Red Cross minimum is 7 days.24 Compare your count with the 84-day average from Maria.2

  3. Count water days

    Asheville's water crisis lasted 53 days.15 Write down how long your stored water lasts. See Storing Water for Months & Years.

  4. Choose a warm room and a cool room

    Cold caused about two-thirds of the Texas 2021 deaths.3 Pick the room now, not during the storm.

  5. Mark the generator spot

    Outdoors, at least 6 m (20 ft) from windows, with CO alarms that have battery backup inside.17

  6. Put cash and paper in the kit

    ATMs and card machines may not work.17 Add printed phone numbers and a written medicine list.

  7. Name the neighbors you will check

    Poor and elderly people had the highest risk after Maria.7 Write down who you will visit first.

🐕 Dogs & pets

Dogs live through the same outage

The Red Cross lists a collar, leash, ID, food, carrier and bowl for pets.24 Count your dog's food and water in every supply total. See Dogs in a Long Emergency.

🎯 Practice now

Run a one-day blackout drill

While help still exists, switch off the power at the main switch for one day. Note what fails first: water, cooking, light, phones or medicine. Fix those gaps before anything else.

🛒 Get it before you need it

Get these before you need them

A CO alarm with battery backup,17 flashlights and spare batteries,17 lidded water containers, cash in small notes,24 and extra supplies of every daily medicine your doctor agrees to.

Download these while the internet works

Ready.gov: Power Outages, the HHS emPOWER FAQ on electricity-dependent patients, and the Hurricane Maria mortality study (NEJM, PDF).
