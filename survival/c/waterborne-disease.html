#🦠 Why these diseases matter so much

Diarrhea is one of the top killers of young children. Diarrheal disease is the third leading cause of death in children aged 1–59 months.1

Each year it kills about 443,832 children under 5 and another 50,851 children aged 5 to 9.1 There are nearly 1.7 billion cases of childhood diarrheal disease every year.1

Water is a main route. The World Health Organization (WHO) says contaminated drinking water can spread diarrhea, cholera, dysentery, typhoid and polio.2 It causes about 505,000 diarrheal deaths each year.2

A 2023 WHO report added up the cost of unsafe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). In 2019, safe WASH could have prevented at least 1.4 million deaths.3

More than 1 million of those deaths were from diarrhea.3

📊 Key fact

Children pay the highest price

Among children under 5, unsafe WASH caused 395,000 deaths in 2019.3 Diarrhea caused 273,000 of them.3

⏳ The 30-year view

The 30-year view

In a grid-down life, your household does the work of the water plant, the sewer and the first clinic, for decades. This chapter is the "why". Disinfecting Water, Toilets Without Water and Handwashing & Personal Hygiene are the "how".

Learn the first signs and how long each germ takes to show. Use the table as a quick guide. The details follow below it.

Cholera

Cholera can kill within hours. WHO calls it a severe diarrheal disease that can kill within hours if it is not treated.4

You catch it by drinking or eating something that contains cholera bacteria. Symptoms usually start 2–3 days later, but can start within a few hours or as late as 5 days.6

Early signs are watery diarrhea, vomiting, leg cramps and restlessness. The diarrhea often looks milky white and is called "rice-water stool".6

About 1 in 10 people get severe symptoms. Untreated severe dehydration can lead to kidney failure, shock, coma and death.6

☠️ Danger

Cholera: act in hours, not days

If someone has large amounts of watery diarrhea, start ORS immediately and get them to any working clinic. With early and proper treatment, even people with severe cholera can survive.6

Typhoid fever

Typhoid spreads through sewage. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) says it spreads through food, drinks and water contaminated by sewage, and through food rinsed in contaminated water.9

Symptoms usually appear 6–30 days after infection.10 They include fever, headache and stomach problems such as constipation or diarrhea.910

Feeling better does not mean the danger is over. CDC says people can keep carrying and spreading the bacteria after their symptoms go away.9

Dysentery (bloody diarrhea)

Dysentery is diarrhea with blood in it. WHO calls acute bloody diarrhea dysentery.1 It lists dysentery among the diseases that contaminated drinking water spreads.2

Blood in the stool is a reason to get professional care. See when diarrhea becomes an emergency in Diarrhea, Dehydration & Oral Rehydration.

E. coli

E. coli is a leading cause of serious diarrhea. WHO names rotavirus and Escherichia coli (E. coli) as the two most common causes of moderate-to-severe diarrhea in low-income countries.1

It spreads through sewage. CDC warns that floodwater can contain sewage, and that swallowing water or food it has contaminated can cause diarrheal disease such as E. coli infection.20

Boiling and chlorine both kill bacteria such as E. coli.19 Treat it like any diarrhea: ORS first, and professional care if the stool turns bloody.

Giardia

Giardia is a parasite that causes long, draining diarrhea. Symptoms usually start 1–2 weeks after you swallow the germs and last 2–6 weeks.11

Look for diarrhea, gas, smelly greasy stools that can float, stomach cramps, nausea and dehydration.11

Symptoms can last longer than 6 weeks, especially in people with weak immune systems. Some infected people have no symptoms at all.11

Cryptosporidium ("Crypto")

Crypto is the germ that chlorine does not stop. Its tough shells (oocysts) can survive 3.5–10.6 days in water with 1–3 mg/L (1–3 ppm) of free chlorine.13 CDC rates iodine and chlorine as not effective against it.19

Illness starts on average 7 days after exposure, with a range of 2–10 days.12 In healthy people it usually clears up within 2–3 weeks.14

In people with weak immune systems it can cause watery diarrhea for weeks to months, and even life-threatening malnutrition and wasting.14

⚠️ Caution

Bleach alone is not enough for Crypto

Boil the water: a rolling boil for 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 2,000 m (6,500 ft).5 CDC rates boiling as very effective against parasites such as Crypto.19

No fuel? A filter with an absolute pore size of 1 micron or smaller removes Crypto.19 Compare your options in Water Filters & SODIS and Disinfecting Water.

Hepatitis A

Hepatitis A is a liver virus that spreads from stool to mouth. It passes from person to person, or through contaminated food or water.16

Symptoms include tiredness, nausea or vomiting, stomach pain, fever, joint pain, loss of appetite, dark urine, clay-colored stools and jaundice (yellow skin or eyes).17

Most young children show nothing. About 70% of infections in children under 6 cause no symptoms,16 so you cannot tell who carries it by looking.

  1. Day 0

    The virus is swallowed in food or water, or passed on by close contact with an infected person.16

  2. Day 15–50

    Symptoms begin, on average around day 28. Many young children never show any.16

  3. While ill

    Tiredness, nausea, stomach pain, dark urine, clay-colored stools, yellow skin or eyes.17

  4. Under 2 months

    Symptoms usually last less than 2 months.17

#⏱️ Water or food? Use the clock

The same germs travel by water, food and hands. The true source is always someone's stool.

CDC lists food, drinks and water contaminated by sewage as typhoid routes.9 For hepatitis A it lists close contact and contaminated food or water.16

So the useful question is: which water, which food, which hands? The incubation period, the time from swallowing a germ to the first symptom, tells you when the exposure happened.

Do the arithmetic. If a child's first hepatitis A symptoms start on day 60, the exposure was between day 10 and day 45 (60 minus 50, and 60 minus 15).16

If cholera-like diarrhea starts on day 60, look at what people drank and ate from day 55 to day 60.6

Look-back windows: if symptoms started today, search this far back
Suspected diseaseLook backMost likely
CholeraA few hours to 5 days62–3 days ago6

Hepatitis A15–50 days16About 4 weeks (28 days) ago16

Long windows mean a wide search. For typhoid and hepatitis A, the source can be a meal or a water run from weeks ago, so a written log matters more than memory.

Step by step · 6 steps

  1. Write it down

    For every sick person, note the day and hour of the first symptom, and what the symptoms are.

  2. Match the disease

    Use the table above to find the most likely germ.

  3. Count back

    Subtract the incubation range from the first-symptom day. That gives the exposure window.

  4. List shared sources

    Every water source, drink, shared dish and food handler in that window.

  5. Compare sick and well

    The source the sick people shared and the well people did not is your lead suspect.

  6. Cut it off

    Stop using it. Treat all drinking and rinse water. See Epidemics: Quarantine, Isolation & Infection Control for caring for the sick safely.

💡 Tip

Clues: water, food or hands?

  • Water: the sick people all drank from one source, even if they ate different meals.
  • Food: the sick people all ate one dish, whatever they drank. See Food Safety Without a Refrigerator.
  • Hands and close contact: cases follow one another inside a household. Hepatitis A spreads from person to person.16
  • No germ pattern fits: think about fuel, pesticides or other chemicals. See Chemical Contamination.

#🚰 Break the chain: toilets, water, hands, food

Every disease in this chapter starts in someone's stool. Typhoid travels through sewage,9 and hepatitis A moves from stool to mouth.16 Block each route and the germ cannot reach a mouth.

Hands matter as much as water. WHO linked about 384,000 diarrheal deaths in 2019 to poor hand hygiene alone.3 It linked another 356,000 deaths from respiratory infections to poor hand hygiene.3

The four barriers

  • Toilet: keep stool far from water and food. See Toilets Without Water for distances from wells.
  • Treat water: every drop for drinking, brushing teeth, mixing baby formula and rinsing food. See Disinfecting Water.
  • Wash hands: after the toilet and diaper changes, and before touching food. See Handwashing & Personal Hygiene.
  • Food: rinse it in treated water only. CDC lists food rinsed in contaminated water as a typhoid route.9

Chlorinated water is safe from every germ.

Cryptosporidium survives 3.5–10.6 days in water held at 1–3 mg/L of free chlorine.13 Chlorine still kills most bacteria and viruses; for Crypto, boil or use a 1-micron filter.19

Do

  • Drink, cook and brush teeth with treated water only
  • Rinse fruit and vegetables in treated water9
  • Wash hands after the toilet, diapers and caring for the sick
  • Keep the toilet away from wells and streams
  • Start ORS at the first watery stool

Don't

  • Rely on bleach alone for Cryptosporidium13
  • Let anyone with diarrhea cook for others
  • Let someone who had typhoid cook for others without a clinician's advice9
  • Use one dipper for both raw and treated water
  • Guess with antibiotics

#💧 Treatment: oral rehydration first

Replace lost fluid first. That is what keeps people alive. WHO says most people with cholera have mild or moderate diarrhea and can be treated with ORS.4

CDC adds that with early, proper treatment, even people with severe cholera can survive.6

Start at the first watery stools. WHO defines diarrhea as 3 or more loose or liquid stools in a day.1

For children with diarrhea, WHO and UNICEF recommend low-osmolarity ORS plus zinc supplements.18 Zinc shortens an episode of diarrhea by 25%.1 Take the zinc dose from a clinician or the package label. The full method and home recipe are in Diarrhea, Dehydration & Oral Rehydration.

Step by step · 5 steps

  1. Start ORS right away

    Give small sips often. Use sachets, or the recipe in the ORS chapter.18

  2. Keep feeding

    Babies keep breastfeeding. Children keep eating their normal food.18

  3. Check for dehydration

    Fast heartbeat, dry mouth, skin that is slow to flatten after a pinch.6

  4. Protect everyone else

    One carer, one toilet or bucket for the sick person, handwashing after every contact, and no cooking for others.

  5. Get professional care for danger signs

    Use the table in the next section. Go early, not late.

Caring for a sick person without spreading it

The carer is the person most exposed. Hepatitis A spreads by close contact,16 and typhoid can spread even after the patient feels better.9 Set up the sickroom before you need it.

  • One carer where possible, who washes hands after every contact, every bucket and every diaper.
  • One toilet or lidded bucket for the sick person, emptied into the latrine, never near water.
  • Separate cup, plate and towel, washed apart from the family's.
  • Soiled bedding and clothes handled with gloves and washed away from the water source.
  • No food preparation by the sick person or, ideally, by the carer.

Disinfectant strengths for surfaces and laundry are in Epidemics: Quarantine, Isolation & Infection Control. Day-to-day nursing is in Nursing the Sick at Home.

Cholera is a death sentence without a hospital.

Most cholera patients can be treated with ORS.4 Early treatment is what matters; even severe cases can survive with it.6

☠️ Danger

Do not guess with antibiotics

Never give leftover, fish or animal antibiotics. ORS comes first; antibiotics are a clinician's decision. Read the facts in Infections, Sepsis & Antibiotic Facts and get a clinician's advice wherever one exists.

#🚨 Danger signs: get help fast

Move fast when fluid loss is heavy. Cholera can kill within hours.4 Other diseases are slower, but still need professional care.

#👪 Protect the people most at risk

The same germ hits some people much harder. Plan your best water, your ORS and your fastest trip to care around them.

👶 Babies & children

Babies and young children

Diarrhea kills about 443,832 children under 5 every year.1 In 2019, unsafe WASH caused 273,000 diarrheal deaths in this age group.3

Wash hands after every diaper change: 70% of young children with hepatitis A show no symptoms.16 Mix formula only with safe water (see Feeding Babies When Formula Runs Out).

🧓 Elderly & disabled

Elderly people and weak immune systems

In people with weak immune systems, Crypto can cause diarrhea for weeks to months and life-threatening wasting.14 Even nitazoxanide, a drug used for Crypto, has not been shown to work in these patients.15

Giardia can also last beyond 6 weeks in them.11 Give them boiled water, because boiling kills Crypto,19 and watch them closely for dehydration.

🌸 Women & girls

Pregnant women

If you are pregnant and get heavy diarrhea, vomiting, fever or yellow eyes, get professional care as early as you can. Keep drinking ORS meanwhile, and see Pregnancy Care Without a Hospital.

🐕 Dogs & pets

Dogs

Your dog shares your floor, your hands and often your water. Give dogs clean water, keep them out of the latrine and away from the drinking-water source, and bury or bag their feces away from water. See Rabies & Diseases Animals Give Humans.

#⏳ The long haul: outbreaks keep coming

Cholera is not a disease of the past. In 2024, 60 countries reported 560,823 cholera cases and 6,028 deaths to WHO.8

Cholera deaths rose for the second year in a row: 2024 deaths were about 50% higher than in 2023.7 By WHO's September 2025 report, 31 countries had reported outbreaks that year.7

⏳ The 30-year view

Keep a sickness log for 30 years

A paper notebook beats memory. Record each case: name, date of first symptom, symptoms, and what the person drank and ate. It turns the arithmetic above into a real early warning. See Public Health for a Small Community.

🎯 Practice now

Vaccinate while clinics still exist

CDC recommends typhoid vaccination for travelers to high-risk areas.9 Hepatitis A is a vaccine-preventable disease covered in CDC's vaccine reference, the Pink Book.16 Ask a clinician now which vaccines fit your family.

🛒 Get it before you need it

Get these before you need them

  • ORS sachets, low-osmolarity formula18
  • Zinc for children, to use as a clinician or label directs18
  • A water filter with an absolute pore size of 1 micron or smaller, which removes Cryptosporidium, plus spare parts19
  • Fuel set aside only for boiling drinking water
  • A thermometer, gloves and a notebook for the sickness log
  • A lidded bucket set aside for a sick person

#🧠 Remember this

How soon can cholera cause symptoms?

Within a few hours to 5 days; usually 2–3 days.

What do cholera stools look like?

Milky white, watery "rice-water" stools.

What is the first treatment for watery diarrhea?

Oral rehydration solution (ORS), started right away.

Does chlorine kill Cryptosporidium?

Not reliably. It survives 3.5–10.6 days at 1–3 mg/L free chlorine. Boil the water instead.

Hepatitis A incubation?

15–50 days, average 28.

Classic Giardia signs?

Gas, cramps, smelly greasy floating stools, 1–2 weeks after exposure.

Deaths linked to unsafe WASH in 2019?

At least 1.4 million, over 1 million of them from diarrhea.
