⚡ The 30-second version

  • Plan in four phases, not one stockpile. Live on stores (year 0–1), build production (years 1–3), make it last (years 3–10), then rebuild and hand on (years 10–30). One person at 2,100 kcal a day eats about 23 million kcal in 30 years. No home can store that.10
  • Year 1 is the build year. Puerto Rico needed about 11 months just to restore power after Hurricane Maria.13 Start every long-term system while stored food still covers you.
  • Everything stored has an end date. Fridge food about 4 hours, insulin out of the fridge up to 28 days, home-bottled water 6 months, bleach 1 year, CO alarms 5 years.35267
  • Protect the children born after the grid fails. Diarrhea is the third leading cause of death in children aged 1–59 months worldwide.17 Clean water, toilets and handwashing are your child survival plan.
  • Skills outlast supplies. The AHA says children 12 and older can learn CPR.18 Drill twice a year, every year.19

#🗺️ The 30-year plan on one page

This chapter turns the whole book into a schedule. For each phase it shows what to work on, what runs out, which skills to master and how your family changes.

Stored supplies are a bridge, not a plan. At the planning figure of 2,100 kcal a day, one person eats 22,995,000 kcal in 30 years.10 At the CDC minimum of 1 gallon a day, they need 10,950 gallons (about 41,450 liters) of water.2

Real outages already last months. Homes surveyed in Puerto Rico after Hurricane Maria averaged 84 days without power.12 In Ukraine, attacks cut dispatchable power capacity from about 38 GW before 2022 to about 12 GW after spring 2024.14

This book plans for the longer case: 30 years with no grid and no repair crews. For the real events in detail, read Real Long Blackouts.

Long outages end in a few days, so a 3-day kit is enough.

Puerto Rico homes averaged 84 days without power after Maria,12 and the whole island took about 11 months.13 The Red Cross 3-day supply is for evacuation; it advises 2 weeks at home.1

#📦 Phase 1 · Year 0–1: Live on stores, build systems Year 1

Your job in year 1 is to stretch what you have and start what replaces it. Do not wait for stores to run low. The first weeks are covered in The First 72 Hours and The First 30 Days.

What runs out in Phase 1

  1. Hour 0

    ATMs and card machines may not work. Ready.gov says to keep some cash.4

  2. Hour 4

    Food in a closed fridge stays safe for about 4 hours.3

  3. Day 1–2

    A full freezer holds about 48 hours, a half-full one about 24.3 Refrigerated medicine: discard after more than a day without power unless the label says otherwise.4

  4. Day 14

    The end of a Red Cross 2-week home supply.1

  5. Day 28

    Insulin vials and cartridges can stay out of the fridge for up to 28 days, and only at 15–30°C (59–86°F).5

  6. Month 6

    Water you bottled yourself is due for replacement.2

  7. Month 12

    Bleach passes the 1-year age that EPA's water disinfection method uses.6

Ration food, never water. FEMA and the Red Cross say healthy people can live on half their usual food for an extended period, but children and pregnant women should not be rationed.11

A normally active person needs to drink at least 2 quarts (about 2 liters) a day. Drink what you need today and find more for tomorrow.11

Do the year math once, on paper. A family of 4 at 2,100 kcal each eats 3,066,000 kcal a year.10 Add up the kcal printed on your stored food, divide by your family's yearly number, and you know how much of the year your stores cover.

Food calculator. Interactive in the digital edition.

Set up the year-1 systems

Step by step · 6 steps

  1. Count everything

    Food in kcal, water in liters, medicine in days. Write the totals on the wall.

  2. Set the ration

    Half portions for healthy adults only if needed. Full portions for children and pregnant women.11

  3. Build the water system

    A source, a way to treat it and clean storage. Read Rainwater Harvesting and Disinfecting Water.

  4. Build the toilet system

    Do it before stored supplies of bags and wipes run out. Read Composting Toilets.

  5. Plant the first beds

    Check days to harvest on each seed packet. Plan food for the gap until harvest. Read Survival Gardening.

  6. Write a plan for each daily medicine

    List the medicine, the dose and the days left. Read Chronic Conditions Without Pharmacies.

📊 Key fact

Missed medical care killed slowly in Puerto Rico

About one third of the excess deaths after Hurricane Maria were linked to delayed or interrupted health care.12 Researchers found the poor and the elderly at highest risk.25 Medical continuity is a Phase 1 job, not a later one.

🧓 Elderly & disabled

Cold medicines and powered devices

If a person's life depends on a refrigerated medicine, Ready.gov says to ask a doctor or pharmacist and keep using it until a new supply is available.4

Make that plan with the doctor now, before the outage. Read Caring for Elderly & Disabled People.

☠️ Danger

Carbon monoxide kills in every phase

Run generators outdoors, more than 20 feet (6 m) from windows, doors and vents, and never in a home or garage.7 Fit battery CO alarms near sleeping areas.7 Read Carbon Monoxide.

#🌱 Phase 2 · Years 1–3: Production replaces purchases Long haul

In Phase 2, anything you cannot grow, make or repair becomes a countdown. Your job is to replace each store with a working system before it reaches zero.

Rationing stretches a store. It never refills one. Only production refills it, so production gets your best hours.

If we store enough, we will never need to farm.

At 2,100 kcal a day, a family of 4 eats about 92 million kcal in 30 years (91,980,000).10 Stores buy the time it takes to build production.

Production systems to start by year 2

  • Staple crops and seed: Survival Gardening and Seed Saving
  • Preserving the harvest: Drying, Smoking, Salting & Fermenting and Root Cellars
  • Eggs and meat: Chickens and Rabbits, Goats & Small Livestock
  • Fruit and nut trees: check how many years each tree takes to bear, and plant those first. Read Perennials & Fruit Trees.
⚠️ Caution

Bleach and iodine are first-year tools

EPA's method uses unscented bleach stored at room temperature for less than a year.6 CDC does not recommend iodine for continuous use beyond a few weeks, or for pregnant women or people with thyroid problems.9

For decades of safe water, plan on boiling, filtering and good sources.

🐕 Dogs & pets

Dogs in the production years

CDC says to store extra water for pets.2 Carbon monoxide poisons animals as well as people.7 The Red Cross pet list includes a collar, leash, ID, food, a carrier and a bowl.1

By Phase 2, plan to feed dogs from your own production. Read Feeding Dogs Without Kibble.

#🔧 Phase 3 · Years 3–10: Make it last Long haul

Phase 3 is maintenance. Your systems now feed you. The danger shifts to things that wear out slowly: alarms, plastics, tools, skills and health.

What wears out in Phase 3

  • CO alarms: CDC says to replace them every 5 years.7 The alarms you fit in year 0 are due in year 5.
  • Plastic drums in the sun: Be Ready Utah says blue barrels in direct sun can start to fall apart within 5 years.8 Shade them from day one.
  • Medicines: every medicine has a date. The WHO 2025 Model Lists name 523 essential medicines for adults and 374 for children.24 Read Medicines: Shelf Life.
  • Kits: Ready.gov says to re-think your needs every year and update the kit as your family changes.23 A newborn from year 0 is a school-age child by year 6.

Disease risk rises with crowding and poor sanitation. A CDC review of epidemics after disasters linked outbreak risk to displacement, crowding, water and sanitation, nutrition, vaccine immunity and access to health care.15

WHO reports that better water, sanitation and hygiene can save about 1.4 million lives a year (2019 estimates).16 Poor hand hygiene alone was linked to about 384,000 diarrhea deaths.16 Read Public Health for a Small Community.

Do

  • Train two people for every critical job
  • Keep a written log for each system
  • Shade plastic drums and tanks
  • Replace alarms on schedule
  • Teach children something every day

Don't

  • Let one person hold a skill nobody else has
  • Put off small repairs
  • Use food-grade drums for fuel or chemicals
  • Stop drilling once life feels normal
⏳ The 30-year view

Phase 3 builds the group, not just the farm

Shared work, shared food and disputes all need rules that everyone knows. Write your group's rules early, while things are calm. Read Group Rules, Decisions & Disputes and Tool Maintenance.

#🏗️ Phase 4 · Years 10–30: Rebuild and hand it on Long haul

Phase 4 is about handing knowledge on. The adults who learned skills before the grid failed are aging. The children born after it failed are growing into the workforce.

The math is simple addition. A baby born in year 1 turns 18 in year 19. An adult who is 50 at the start is 80 in year 30.

Older people need planned care, not leftover care. After Hurricane Maria, the poor and the elderly were at highest risk of death.25 Ready.gov says older adults and young children are especially vulnerable to extreme temperatures.4

Phase 4 jobs

  • Trades with apprentices: Rebuilding Local Trades
  • Power without the grid: Low-Tech Power
  • Records that last: Preserving Knowledge & Records
  • Teaching the next generation: Passing On Skills
⏳ The 30-year view

Year 30 is a checkpoint, not an end

At one replacement every 5 years,7 each sleeping area uses 6 CO alarms in 30 years. A home with 3 sleeping areas uses 18.

Every habit in this chapter, from the daily water log to the twice-yearly drill, carries on past year 30.

#👶 Children born after the grid fails

Plan for babies to be born in your group. Treat pregnancy, birth and childhood as core systems, not surprises. For methods and how well each works, read Contraception & Family Planning.

Young children die mostly of preventable causes. WHO lists diarrhea as the third leading cause of death in children aged 1–59 months, with about 443,832 deaths in children under 5 each year.17

WHO estimates that unsafe water, sanitation and hygiene caused about 395,000 deaths of children under 5 in 2019.16 Your water and toilet systems are your children's survival systems.

Vaccinate everyone now and copy the records. After disasters, measles risk depends on vaccine coverage, especially among children under 15.15 The Red Cross says to keep copies of important documents.1 Read Vaccine-Preventable Diseases.

👶 Babies & children

Stored water drowns toddlers

From 2006 to 2010, CPSC counted 434 in-home drowning deaths of children under 5. 82% were under age 2, and 81% involved bathtubs or bath products.22 CPSC warns about buckets, too.22

A home that stores water for decades needs a lid on every bucket, drum and tank, every day.

🌸 Women & girls

Pregnancy and breastfeeding come first

The protein RDA rises to 71 g a day in pregnancy and breastfeeding.21 FEMA and the Red Cross say not to ration food for pregnant women.11 CDC says to store extra water for them.2

Read Pregnancy Care, Emergency Childbirth and Breastfeeding before anyone in your group is pregnant.

A child's path through the 30 years

  • Ages 0–5: safe water, clean hands, toilets, warmth and full food rations.11
  • Ages 6–11: reading, writing, math and safe chores. Read Teaching Kids Without Schools.
  • Age 12 and up: CPR and first aid training.18 Read Survival Skills Kids Can Learn by Age.
  • Late teens: apprenticeship in one trade and one health skill.
🐕 Dogs & pets

Plan the dog line, too

The dogs you start with age along with everyone else. Decide early whether your group will breed dogs, and keep a record of each dog's parents. Read Breeding, Whelping & Dog Population Control.

#🎓 Skills to master in each phase

Supplies run out. Skills do not. Learn the Phase 1 skills now, while trainers, clinics and official courses still exist.

🎯 Practice now

Practice while help still exists

The AHA published its 2025 CPR guidelines on October 22, 2025, and says children 12 and older can learn CPR.18 NFPA says to practice a home escape plan twice a year, by day and at night.19

Most families do not practice. In 2023, only 37% of US adults had made an emergency plan in the past year, down from 41% in 2022.20

🛒 Get it before you need it

Buy for the long phases now

Choose things you can repair or reuse, such as hand tools, metal pots and wool clothing. Buy spare CO alarms and write the date on each one, because each lasts 5 years.7 Read What Fails When before you spend.

Run the twice-yearly review

Step by step · 5 steps

  1. Count

    Recount food, water, medicine, seed, fuel and spare parts. Write the date and the totals.

  2. Compare

    Check your phase's milestone list. Mark what is done and what is behind.

  3. Fix one gap

    Pick the gap that could kill someone soonest. Give it an owner and a deadline.

  4. Drill

    Practice one emergency, such as a fire escape, CPR or a sick child at night.19

  5. Update the plan

    Add each person's new age and role. Ready.gov says to update your kit as your family changes.23

#🧠 Remember this

What are the four phases?

Stores (year 0–1), production (1–3), make it last (3–10), rebuild and hand on (10–30).

How many kcal does one person need in 30 years?

About 23 million, at 2,100 kcal a day.

Who should never be put on short food rations?

Children and pregnant women. And nobody rations water.

How often do you replace a CO alarm?

Every 5 years: 6 alarms per sleeping area in 30 years.

How long can insulin vials stay out of the fridge?

Up to 28 days, at 15–30°C (59–86°F).

Third leading cause of death in children aged 1–59 months?

Diarrhea. Prevent it with safe water, toilets and handwashing.

From what age can children learn CPR?

12 and older (AHA, 2025).
